  01 / The strategy behind the strategy
  A hook wins attention. A persuasion structure makes the idea understandable. A brand position creates
  preference. A unique mechanism explains why the product is different. A conversion path turns interest into
  action.


  The four-part ISSUE11 editorial engine
   • 1. Psychological revelation: reveal a surprising, credible insight about desire, identity, or behavior.

   • 2. Paradigm shift: contrast passive wishing with imagination plus action.

   • 3. Movement manifesto: articulate what the audience believes and what it refuses to accept.

   • 4. Unique mechanism: demonstrate the ISSUE11 experience, not just a promise.


  Core positioning
  ISSUE11 is a personal editorial experience that helps people clarify what they want, imagine a possible future,
  and take meaningful steps toward it. Avoid claims that visualization guarantees outcomes or that the app
  clinically treats mental health conditions.


  The content-to-product bridge
  Insight post → saved/shared carousel → profile visit → short product demo → quiz or landing page → app
  trial/subscription → first meaningful action → weekly editorial ritual.

  05 / Psychological science: how to use it responsibly
  Strong editorial content can be research-informed without pretending that every provocative headline is a
  proven scientific law. Distinguish a documented finding, a plausible interpretation, and your own philosophy.


  Mental contrasting
  Research associated with Gabriele Oettingen examines combining positive future imagery with awareness of
  present obstacles. Avoid claiming that visualization itself is always harmful.


  Implementation intentions
  Peter Gollwitzer's if-then planning research offers a practical way to connect goals with cues and actions.
  Effectiveness varies by context.


  Self-determination theory
  Deci and Ryan's framework emphasizes autonomy, competence, and relatedness. Use it to guide supportive
  rather than controlling messages.


  Possible selves
  Work by Hazel Markus and Paula Nurius explores how imagined future identities relate to motivation; avoid
  equating imagining with guaranteed behavior change.


  Goal-setting theory
  Locke and Latham emphasize conditions under which specific, challenging goals and feedback support
  performance. Goals need context and feasibility.




ISSUE11 / MARKETING & PERSUASION PLAYBOOK                                                                         8
  Habit formation
  Repeated behavior in stable contexts can increase automaticity; the time required varies widely. Avoid “21
  days to rewire your brain” claims.


  Evidence hierarchy for publishing
   • Best: meta-analyses, systematic reviews, well-designed peer-reviewed studies.

   • Good: foundational theory papers and replicated experimental findings, with caveats.

   • Use carefully: single studies, popular books, creator summaries, neuroscience metaphors.

   • Never present an editorial metaphor as a clinical fact; link the original source and note limitations.


  Starter reading / primary research anchors
   • Gollwitzer (1999), “Implementation intentions: Strong effects of simple plans,” American Psychologist.

   • Oettingen (2012), “Future thought and behaviour change,” European Review of Social Psychology.

   • Deci & Ryan (2000), “The what and why of goal pursuits,” Psychological Inquiry.

   • Markus & Nurius (1986), “Possible selves,” American Psychologist.

   • Locke & Latham (2002), “Building a practically useful theory of goal setting,” American Psychologist.

   • Lally et al. (2010), “How are habits formed,” European Journal of Social Psychology.

  08 / A practical 30-day publishing system
  Week 1: Belief disruption
  2 psychological revelations, 1 myth-vs-reality, 1 provocative short video. CTA: save or share.


  Week 2: New paradigm
  2 old-vs-new carousels, 1 movement manifesto, 1 founder story. CTA: follow and join the conversation.

  Week 3: Mechanism and demonstration
  2 app walkthroughs, 1 sample magazine issue, 1 obstacle-to-action tutorial. CTA: visit the landing page.


  Week 4: Proof and conversion
  2 authentic user stories or beta observations, 1 FAQ, 1 clear offer, 1 editorial recap. CTA: try ISSUE11.


  Track by purpose, not vanity metrics alone
   • Attention: 3-second retention, hook hold rate, reach.

   • Value: saves, shares, meaningful comments, completion rate.

   • Interest: profile visits, qualified landing-page sessions.

   • Conversion: install-to-onboarding, onboarding-to-paid, subscription conversion.

   • Retention: first action completed, weekly return rate, magazine creation, churn.


  Testing protocol
  Test one variable at a time when feasible: headline, opening visual, psychological angle, or CTA. Record
  hypothesis, creative, audience, metric, result, and next iteration. Avoid treating tiny samples as proof.

  11 / Reusable editorial brief template
   • Audience tension: What does she want, fear, or misunderstand?

   • Framework: Which persuasion structure fits this tension?

   • Provocation: What surprising but defensible sentence opens the post?

   • Evidence: What source supports the factual claim? What does it NOT prove?

   • Diagram: What is the simplest visual model?

   • Reframe: What new belief should the reader consider?

   • Agency: What one practical action can she take?

   • Product connection: How does ISSUE11 operationalize the idea?

   • CTA: Save, share, comment, visit, try, or return?

   • Success metric: What behavior would show this worked?


  Final editorial principle
  Do not sell a fantasy of instant change. Sell the beauty of participating in your own future. That is a stronger
  promise, a more defensible philosophy, and a more distinctive brand.

  13 / Behavioral psychology and motivation
  STEPPS (Jonah Berger)
  Principle: Six properties that can encourage word of mouth: social currency, triggers, emotion, public visibility, practical value,
  stories.

  ISSUE11: Create a shareable diagram: “What you imagine / what you rehearse / what you actually do.”

  Best for: Saveable carousels, referral concepts


  EAST (Behavioural Insights Team)
  Principle: Make a desired action Easy, Attractive, Social and Timely.

  ISSUE11: Offer one 2-minute weekly action immediately after a magazine reveal.

  Best for: Onboarding, activation


  Fogg Behavior Model
  Principle: A behavior occurs when motivation, ability and a prompt coincide.

  ISSUE11: Ask for a tiny action while the user is inspired, not a 40-minute planning session.

  Best for: Product activation


  COM-B
  Principle: Behavior depends on capability, opportunity and motivation.

  ISSUE11: Diagnose whether the user lacks a skill, time/resources or desire before recommending action.

  Best for: Adaptive coaching


  Self-Determination Theory
  Principle: Autonomy, competence and relatedness are important supports for intrinsic motivation.

  ISSUE11: Let users choose their own next action and see meaningful progress.

  Best for: Retention, tone


  Psychological Reactance
  Principle: Threats to perceived freedom can produce resistance.

  ISSUE11: Say “Try this experiment” instead of “Everything you believed was wrong.”

  Best for: Contrarian copy

 11 / The strategic emphasis
 THE SIGNATURE IS NOT 80 FRAMEWORKS. IT IS FOUR IDEAS REPEATED WITH DISCIPLINE.

 ISSUE11 is not simply a manifestation app. It is an editorial and behavioral practice built around desire, imagination,
 action, and evolution. The content should make readers feel intellectually awakened and practically capable.


 01 / Psychological Revelation — PRIMARY
 Lead with a provocative question, then accurately explain a specific psychological finding, its limits, and one practical
 implication. Make diagrams a recognizable editorial asset.


 02 / Paradigm Shift — PRIMARY
 Reframe manifestation from passive outcome expectation to imagination plus adaptive action. Preserve wonder; reject
 guarantees.


 03 / Movement Manifesto — PRIMARY
 Name the shared values: ambition without shame, imagination without passivity, agency without perfectionism.


 04 / Unique Mechanism — PRIMARY
 Show Desire → Imagine → Act → Evolve through a real product demonstration: personal magazine, journal, weekly
 action, and reflection.


 The editorial non-negotiables
 • Challenge doctrines, not dreamers. Never shame someone for wanting a different life.

 • Do not use a neuroscientific claim unless it has a credible source and an appropriate caveat.

 • Each educational post needs a useful insight; each conversion post needs a concrete product demonstration.

 • Use the same recognizable typography, palette, diagram grammar, and editorial voice across formats.

 • A strong ending gives agency: a question, reflection, experiment, or small action.

 16 / Operating system for AI-assisted editorial
 production
 Research
 Collect the original study or a trustworthy synthesis; note population, design, limitations, and confidence.


 Thesis
 Write one provocative but defensible takeaway. Distinguish findings from interpretation.


 Structure
 Select the goal, audience-awareness level, primary framework, and supporting framework.

 Create a diagram that explains, not decorates. Label illustrative numbers as illustrative.

 Independent check for evidence, overclaiming, brand voice, accessibility, and factual errors.
