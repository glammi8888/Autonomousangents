Fix This Before You Spend

Most jewelry ads send people to one of two places: your Google Business Profile or your website. If either is weak, the ad works and the sale still doesn't happen.

  • Google profile: the right primary category, every service you offer (custom design, repairs, appraisals), current hours and real photos of your cases and your work.
  • Website: one page per specialty, a clear way to book an appointment, and your specialty stated in the first line of the homepage.
  • Reviews: recent ones, with replies. A ring buyer reads them before calling.

Pro tip: before running any ad, search your store's name on your phone. Whatever shows up first is what every ad click will see.

The Channels That Bring In Buyers

Google Search Ads

Search ads reach people at the exact moment they type "engagement rings near me" or "jewelry repair Beverly Hills." That intent is why clicks are expensive in this category. Keep campaigns tight: a few high-intent services, your local area and a landing page that matches the search.

Local and Maps Visibility

Many ring searches end on the map. A complete profile with steady reviews does most of the work here, and Google says local results are based on relevance, distance and prominence.

Instagram and Pinterest

Social ads are strong for gifts, fashion jewelry and seasonal pieces, where a beautiful photo can do the selling. For engagement rings, they work better at building recognition than at getting a same-week visit.

Email to Past Customers

Anniversaries, birthdays and cleaning reminders bring people back without paying for the click again. Only email people who agreed to hear from you.

An ad buys the first look. Your listing, your reviews and your website decide the visit.

Where Ads Can't Reach: AI Search

When someone asks ChatGPT "who makes custom engagement rings in Los Angeles," there's no ad slot to bid on. The answer comes from what's written about you across the web: your website, your Google profile, directory listings and reviews. That's what answer engine optimization (AEO) works on: making your specialties, location and proof clear and consistent everywhere AI looks.

Pro tip: ask ChatGPT and Google AI the three questions your best customers would ask. Write down which jewelers get named. That list is your real competition in AI search.

The Rules Jewelry Ads Have to Follow

The FTC's Guides for the Jewelry, Precious Metals and Pewter Industries cover how jewelry can be described, including metal content, gemstone names and terms for lab-grown and treated stones. Those rules apply to ads, product pages and social captions alike. Accurate wording protects you and makes buyers trust you more.

Pro tip: keep one approved description for each stone and metal type you sell, and paste it into every ad and listing. It saves time and keeps you consistent.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best advertising for a jewelry store?

For engagement rings and repairs, Google Search ads and a complete Google Business Profile usually bring the most ready-to-buy visitors. Social ads work better for gifts and fashion jewelry.

What should a jeweler fix before advertising?

The Google Business Profile, the website pages for each specialty and recent reviews. Ads send people there, so weak pages waste the budget.

Can a jewelry store advertise on ChatGPT?

Not in regular answers. ChatGPT recommends jewelers based on information it finds across the web, so clear websites, consistent listings and real reviews matter more than ad spend.

Are there rules for jewelry ads?

Yes. The FTC's Jewelry Guides cover how metals, diamonds and gemstones, including lab-grown and treated stones, can be described in ads and listings.

How do I know which jewelry ads work?

Track visits and sales, not just clicks. Ask every customer how they found you and include AI search as an option.
