Here's how to design one that answers all four, for the client and for the AI tools now sending you clients.

The First Ten Seconds

Above the fold, answer three things: what you are, where you are and why you. "Medical aesthetics in West Hollywood, led by board-certified nurse injectors" says more than "Reveal your radiance." Add one strong image of your actual space or team and a booking button that stays visible as people scroll.

Pro tip: read your homepage headline out loud and ask: could a competitor across town use the exact same sentence? If yes, rewrite it until they couldn't.

One Page Per Treatment

A single "Services" page listing twenty treatments helps no one. Give each main treatment its own page that covers what it treats, who it suits, what a visit feels like, downtime, how long results tend to last and the questions people actually ask. These pages are what clients land on from Google, and they're what AI tools quote when someone asks about a treatment in your city.

Clients don't book a med spa. They book a person they trust for a specific treatment.

Proof That Builds Trust

  • Your providers: names, credentials, training and a real photo, on a page of their own.
  • Before-and-after photos: real clients only, with their written consent, similar lighting and angles, and no editing that changes results.
  • Reviews: real ones, linked to your Google profile. Never written by staff or bought.
  • Honest claims: the FTC expects health claims to be truthful and backed by evidence. "Can soften lines for about three to four months" beats "erase wrinkles."

Pro tip: add a short "what to expect at your first visit" section to every treatment page. Nervous first-timers book when they can picture the appointment.

Booking Without Friction

Use one booking system everywhere, link it from every page, and let people book a consultation without creating an account first. Show your hours and address in the footer of every page. If you offer free consultations, say so near the button.

Built for AI Search (AEO)

Answer engine optimization (AEO) starts on your website. AI tools like ChatGPT and Google AI pull from pages they can read and trust. That means:

  • One clear heading per page and descriptive subheadings.
  • An FAQ on each treatment page with plain, specific answers.
  • Schema markup that states your business type, address, hours and services.
  • Your name, address and phone written exactly the same as on your Google Business Profile.
  • A fast, clean mobile layout.

Pro tip: ask ChatGPT "what does [your med spa] specialize in?" If the answer is vague or wrong, your website isn't saying it clearly enough yet.

Frequently Asked Questions

What pages should a med spa website have?

A clear homepage, one page per main treatment, a provider page, a before-and-after gallery with consented photos, a page about your first visit, an FAQ and a booking page.

Can a med spa post before-and-after photos?

Yes, with the client's written consent. Use real, unedited photos taken in similar lighting, and keep any claims about results realistic.

How does website design affect AI search?

AI tools recommend clinics they can understand. Clear treatment pages, FAQs, schema markup and consistent business details make your med spa easier to recommend.

Should a med spa website have online booking?

Yes. A visible booking button on every page, without forcing people to create an account, makes it easier for clients to commit.

Does my med spa need a full redesign?

Not always. Many clinics can start by adding treatment pages, provider bios, FAQs and schema to their current site.
