A ring that stops people in your case can look like a smudge of grey on your website. Jewelry is small, shiny and reflective, which makes it one of the hardest things to photograph. The good news: you don't need a studio. With soft light, a steady phone and a few habits, your photos can look as good online as your pieces do in person. And the way you name and describe those photos decides whether Google and AI search can find them.

Light Is Everything

Direct sun and ring lights create hot spots on metal and make stones look flat. Shoot near a window with indirect daylight, or diffuse the light through a white sheet, tracing paper or a lightbox. Add a white card opposite the window to bounce light back into the shadows. For diamonds, a small point of light from the side brings out sparkle without blowing out the metal.

Pro tip: photograph at the same time of day for a whole collection. Consistent light makes your product pages look like one brand, not twenty different afternoons.

A Simple Setup That Works

  • Background: white or light grey for product pages, a texture you own (stone, linen, wood) for lifestyle shots.
  • Phone: use the main lens, not ultra-wide, tap to focus on the stone and lower the exposure slightly so the metal doesn't glow.
  • Stability: a small tripod and a 3-second timer.
  • Clean the piece: fingerprints show up far more on camera than in person. Use gloves and a lint-free cloth.

Online, the photo is the piece. Treat it with the same care as the setting.

The Shots Every Piece Needs

  1. A clean front view on a plain background.
  2. A side or profile view (essential for rings and settings).
  3. A close-up of the stone or detail work.
  4. One worn shot for scale, on a hand, ear or neck.
  5. For custom work: a photo of the piece in your studio or on your bench, which tells buyers it's really yours.

Pro tip: photograph every custom piece before pickup, with the client's permission. In a year, that's a portfolio no stock photo can compete with.

Keep It Honest

Retouch dust, scratches on the background and stray reflections. Don't change the stone's color, clarity or size. The FTC's Jewelry Guides cover how metals and gemstones are described, and photos that make a piece look like something it isn't create the same trust problem as a misleading description. Buyers who get exactly what they saw are the ones who leave reviews.

Make Your Photos Findable

Google can't see your ring the way a buyer does. It reads the file name, the alt text and the words around the image. Google's own image SEO guidance recommends descriptive file names, useful alt text and placing images near relevant text. AI search tools read the same page, so a product page with clear photos and accurate descriptions is easier for them to recommend.

  • File name: describe the piece in a few words, separated by hyphens.
  • Alt text: one plain sentence, like "Oval lab-grown diamond solitaire in 14k yellow gold."
  • Page text: metal, stone, size and price range near the photos.
  • Size: compress large images so pages load fast on phones.

Pro tip: search Google Images for "[your city] custom engagement rings." If your pieces don't appear, your file names and alt text are the first things to fix.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I photograph jewelry with my phone?

Yes. Use the main lens, soft indirect light, a tripod and a timer, and lower the exposure slightly so metal doesn't glare.

What is the best background for jewelry photos?

White or light grey for product pages, and a simple texture like stone or linen for lifestyle shots.

How do I make diamonds sparkle in photos?

Use soft overall light with one small light source from the side, keep the stone clean and focus directly on it.

How much should I edit jewelry photos?

Clean up dust and background marks, but never change a stone's color, clarity or size. Photos should show the piece exactly as it is.

Do jewelry photos help SEO?

Yes. Descriptive file names, accurate alt text and clear product details near the images help Google and AI search understand and show your pieces.
