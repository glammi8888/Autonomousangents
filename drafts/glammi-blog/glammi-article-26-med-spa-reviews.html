Why Reviews Matter More Than Ever

Google lists reviews among the signals behind local rankings, and AI tools summarize them when they recommend a business. A steady flow of recent, detailed reviews tells both that you're active and trusted. Ten reviews from last month do more than a hundred from four years ago.

How to Ask (So People Actually Do It)

  • Timing: for injectables, a few days to two weeks after treatment, when results are visible. For facials, the same or next day.
  • Channel: a short text or email with your direct Google review link. Most clients won't search for you.
  • Who asks: the provider who treated them, in person at checkout, then a follow-up message. People say yes to a person.
  • What to say: "If you're happy with your results, would you share a quick review? It helps other people find us." Ask everyone, not just the clients you think will be glowing.

Pro tip: invite clients to mention what they came in for and how they feel now. You can't script their words, but "it helps if you mention the treatment" is a fair, honest nudge.

The best review strategy is a great result and someone remembering to ask.

How to Reply Without Risking Privacy

Under HIPAA, confirming that someone is your patient or discussing their treatment in a public reply can be a disclosure of health information, even if they shared it first. HHS has taken action against practices for revealing patient details in replies to online reviews. Keep replies warm and general.

  • Good: "Thank you so much for the kind words. We love hearing this."
  • Risky: "So glad your lip filler turned out great, Sarah!"
  • For negative reviews: "We're sorry to hear this. Please give us a call so we can talk privately." Then take it offline.

What You Can Never Do

The FTC's final rule on fake reviews, in effect since 2024, bans buying fake reviews, writing reviews posing as clients, offering rewards for positive (or negative) reviews, and suppressing negative ones. Google's policies also forbid "review gating", asking only happy clients or filtering unhappy ones away from your Google page.

  • No staff or family reviews without disclosing the connection.
  • No discounts or freebies in exchange for reviews.
  • No asking only the clients you know loved it.

Putting Reviews to Work

Share standout reviews on your website and social media with the client's permission, and link back to your Google profile. Read reviews monthly for patterns: if three people mention the wait time, that's an operations fix, not a marketing one. AI tools read the same reviews, so specific praise for a treatment or a provider strengthens how they describe you.

Pro tip: ask ChatGPT "what do clients say about [your med spa]?" The answer shows you which of your strengths AI has picked up, and which it hasn't.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can a med spa get more Google reviews?

Ask every client in person, then send a short text or email with your direct Google review link a few days after treatment, once results are visible.

How should a med spa reply to reviews?

Thank the reviewer warmly and generally, without confirming they're a client or mentioning any treatment, and take complaints offline by phone.

Can a med spa offer a discount for reviews?

No. The FTC's rule bans rewards for reviews that depend on what they say, and Google's policies prohibit incentivized reviews.

What should a med spa do about a negative review?

Reply briefly and politely, invite the person to call so you can talk privately, and never share any details about their visit in public.

Do reviews affect AI search recommendations?

Yes. AI tools read reviews when they describe and recommend businesses, so recent, specific reviews help them understand what you're known for.
