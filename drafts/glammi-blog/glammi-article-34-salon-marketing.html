How Luxury Salon Clients Choose Now

Someone paying for a color correction or a full head of extensions doesn't pick the closest salon. They research. They look at photos of the exact service, read reviews for the stylist's name, check whether you do their hair type and then book. More of them now start that research by asking an AI tool, which reads the same profile, reviews and website you already have and turns them into a short list.

So your marketing has two readers: the client and the tools that recommend you to the client. The good news is that both want the same thing. Clear, specific, consistent information about what you do best.

Your Google Profile Is Your Front Window

For a salon, the Google Business Profile often gets more views than the website. Google says local results are based on relevance, distance and prominence, so a complete profile with steady activity is the base of everything else.

  • Pick the right categories. "Hair salon" as the main one, then any extra categories Google offers that match your menu, such as extensions or coloring.
  • List services with real names. "Lived-in blonde," "keratin smoothing," "curly cut." Those are the words people type and ask.
  • Post your own photos every week. Finished work in daylight, the space, the team. Label them by service when you can.
  • Keep hours and booking link current. A wrong holiday schedule costs you the booking and the trust.

Reviews That Sell a $400 Appointment

"Great salon!" doesn't help a nervous first-time client. A review that says "Dana fixed my box-dye orange in one session and kept my hair healthy" does. It also gives Google and AI tools the service, the result and the stylist in one sentence.

  • Ask at checkout, while the client is still looking in the mirror.
  • Send the review link by text the same day, with a nudge like "it helps if you mention the service and who did your hair."
  • Reply to every review using the service name. Short and warm is enough.

Pro tip: never offer a discount for a 5-star review or post reviews yourself. Google's contribution policy bans incentivized and fake reviews, and it removes them.

One Page per Signature Service

If your website has one "Services" page with a price list, search engines can't tell you're the color specialist in your area. Give your two or three signature services their own page each.

Each page should answer what clients ask before booking: who it's for, how long it takes, how often you'll need upkeep, a starting price range, before and after photos and which stylists do it. Add an FAQ at the bottom. This is the content AI tools quote when someone asks "how much is balayage in Beverly Hills" or "salon that specializes in curly hair near me."

Add LocalBusiness structured data to the site with your name, address, phone and hours, matching your Google profile exactly. It helps machines connect your website to your listing.

Getting Named by ChatGPT and Google AI

AI answers about salons usually draw on your Google profile, your site, review sites and local "best of" articles. You can't buy your way into them, but you can make yourself easy to recommend:

  • Say your specialty in one sentence, everywhere. "Color and extensions studio in West Hollywood" on your site, profile, Instagram bio and Yelp.
  • Get mentioned where AI looks. Local magazine roundups, bridal guides and neighborhood blogs carry weight.
  • Check what AI says about you today. Ask ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and Perplexity the questions your clients ask, then fix whatever is missing or wrong.

Where Instagram Fits

Instagram is where clients fall for your work, but it's weak at being found by someone who doesn't follow you yet. Treat it as your portfolio and send people to it from your profile and service pages. Put your city and specialty in the bio and use the same business name as on Google, so the two get connected.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best marketing for a luxury hair salon?

A complete Google Business Profile with weekly photos, reviews that name the service and stylist and one website page for each signature service. Instagram works best as the portfolio those lead to.

What is hair salon SEO?

Hair salon SEO is making your salon easy for Google and AI tools to find and recommend, mainly through your Google profile, reviews, service pages and the same details on every listing.

How do I get more salon reviews?

Ask at checkout, text the review link the same day and ask clients to mention the service and their stylist. Never pay or discount for reviews.

How do I get my salon recommended by ChatGPT?

State your specialty clearly everywhere, earn specific reviews, publish pages that answer client questions and get mentioned in local guides. Then check the answers monthly and fix what's missing.

How long does salon marketing take to work?

Profile updates and new reviews can help within weeks. New website pages usually take a few months to settle in Google, and no one can guarantee a ranking.
