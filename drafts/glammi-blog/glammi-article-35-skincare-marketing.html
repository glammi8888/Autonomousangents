What Changed in How People Buy Skincare

Skincare shoppers have always researched hard. They compare ingredient lists, read reviews from people with their skin type and look for proof before spending $60 on a serum. What's new is where that research starts. More of it now happens inside AI answers, which pull from brand sites, retailer listings, reviews, Reddit threads and beauty editors' roundups, then name a few products.

That rewards brands that are easy to understand. If an AI tool can't tell what your product does, who it's for and why people like it, it recommends someone else.

Product Pages That Answer Skin Questions

Most skincare product pages lead with mood: a pretty photo, a poetic name and three adjectives. Keep the mood, then add the answers underneath:

  • Who it's for. Skin types and concerns in plain words: "oily and combination skin, visible pores, post-breakout marks."
  • Key ingredients and their percentages when you can share them. "10% niacinamide, 1% zinc" is the kind of detail AI quotes.
  • How to use it. Morning or night, where it goes in a routine and what not to mix it with.
  • What to expect. Texture, scent and a realistic timeline, without promising a result.
  • An FAQ with the questions your customer service team answers every week.

Add Product structured data with price, availability and review ratings so search engines can read the page cleanly. Keep the product name identical on your site, Amazon, Sephora or wherever else you sell.

Keep Claims Honest

In the US, a product that claims to treat a condition like acne or change how skin works can be regulated as a drug, not a cosmetic. "Helps skin look smoother" is a cosmetic claim. "Repairs collagen" may not be. AI tools also compare what you say with reviews and other sources, so specific, believable language holds up better than big promises.

Reviews That Name Skin Type and Result

A review like "Love it!" does little. "I have oily, acne-prone skin and this didn't break me out; my marks looked lighter after about six weeks" sells the product and gives AI a skin type, a concern and an outcome in one line.

  • Ask for reviews a few weeks after delivery, once there's something to report.
  • Prompt for skin type and concern in the review form.
  • Show reviews on the product page as text, not only as images, so they can be read.
  • Never write, buy or gate reviews. Real, mixed reviews are more convincing than a wall of five stars.

Get Mentioned Where AI Looks

When AI tools answer product questions, they lean on sources other than you: beauty editors' "best of" lists, dermatologist-led content, retailer pages and real conversations on Reddit. A few practical moves:

  • Pitch your hero product to editors who write roundups for your category, with clear ingredient and price facts.
  • Keep retailer listings as complete as your own site, with the same claims and ingredient details.
  • Join Reddit conversations as the brand, openly, when someone asks about your product. Answer questions; don't sell.
  • Ask AI tools the questions your customers ask and see which sources they cite. That's your outreach list.

Where TikTok and Instagram Fit

Social is where skincare goes viral, and creators showing real skin build trust fast. But a video that does well this week is hard to find in a month. Use social to start demand, then make sure your site, reviews and retailer pages answer the questions those videos raise. That's what search and AI tools find later.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best marketing strategy for a skincare brand?

Clear product pages that answer skin-type questions, honest claims, detailed customer reviews and mentions from editors and retailers. Social media builds demand, and those foundations turn it into sales.

How do skincare brands get recommended by ChatGPT?

By being easy to understand and widely described: specific product pages, consistent details across retailers, reviews that mention skin type and results, plus coverage in sources AI tools cite.

What claims can a skincare brand make?

In the US, cosmetic claims are about appearance, like "helps skin look smoother." Claims to treat a condition or change skin structure can make a product a drug, so check with a regulatory expert.

How do I get better skincare reviews?

Ask a few weeks after delivery, prompt customers to share their skin type and concern and show reviews as readable text on the product page. Never buy or write reviews.

How long does skincare marketing for AI search take?

Fixing product pages and listings can change answers within weeks. Earning editor mentions and steady reviews usually takes a few months, and no one can guarantee placement.
