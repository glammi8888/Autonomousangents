Start With Claims You Can Back Up

Every skincare ad lives or dies on its claim. In the US, a cosmetic claim is about appearance ("helps skin look brighter"). A claim to treat a condition or change how skin works ("treats acne," "repairs collagen") can make a product a drug in the eyes of regulators. Ad platforms also review skincare and health-adjacent ads and can reject ones that overpromise.

Keep claims specific and supportable: name the key ingredient, describe the feel and the routine and, if you have consumer study results, state exactly what was measured and how many people took part. "Brighter-looking skin" with a clear ingredient story tends to outlast "erase dark spots in 7 days."

Creative That Works for Skincare

Skincare is personal and visual, so the ads that do well feel like a friend's recommendation:

  • Real skin, real texture. Close-ups of the product on skin, without heavy filters. Shoppers have learned to distrust perfect skin.
  • Creator videos. A creator with the target skin type showing their routine, what they noticed and how it feels.
  • Texture shots. The serum dropping, the cream spreading. They stop the scroll and answer "what's it like?"
  • One message per ad. One skin concern, one product, one reason to try it.
  • Answers to doubts. "Will it break me out?" "Is it safe with retinol?" Turning common questions into ads often beats a polished brand film.

Which Platform for What

Meta (Instagram and Facebook) is where most skincare brands find new customers with video and carousel ads. TikTok rewards native, creator-style content and can move a hero product fast. Google catches people already searching for a product type ("niacinamide serum for oily skin"), so Shopping and search ads work well once your product pages are strong. Pinterest suits routines and self-care themes with a longer path to purchase.

Most small brands do best starting with one discovery channel (Meta or TikTok) and one intent channel (Google), then expanding only after something works.

The Page After the Click

An ad can't fix a weak product page. When someone clicks, the page should answer within seconds: who the product is for, key ingredients, how to use it, what to expect and what real customers with their skin type say. If the ad promised "for sensitive skin," the page should say so near the top. Fast mobile load times matter more than anything fancy.

Where Ads Meet AI Search

Many shoppers see an ad, then check before buying: they read reviews, search Reddit or ask ChatGPT "is this serum worth it?" If AI tools have little to go on, or describe your product differently than your ad does, that doubt costs you the sale. Consistent product details across your site and retailers, honest reviews that mention skin type and coverage from beauty editors make ads work harder, because the research that follows the ad confirms it.

Testing on a Small Budget

You don't need a big budget to learn what works. Test three to five different creative ideas (not five versions of one idea) with a small daily budget each, give them enough time to gather data, keep the winners and replace the rest. Track cost per purchase and return rate, not just clicks. And keep a simple log of what you tested, so you don't repeat losing ideas.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best platform for skincare advertising?

For most brands, Meta or TikTok to find new customers and Google for people already searching for a product type. Start with one of each and expand once something works.

What claims can skincare ads make?

In the US, cosmetic claims describe appearance, like "helps skin look brighter." Claims to treat a condition or change skin structure can make a product a drug, and ad platforms may reject ads that overpromise.

What kind of skincare ads work best?

Short creator-style videos with real skin, close-up texture shots and ads that answer one common question or concern at a time.

How much should a skincare brand spend on ads?

Start small: test a few distinct creative ideas with a modest daily budget each, keep what lowers your cost per purchase and scale slowly from there.

Do ads help with AI search?

Not directly. But shoppers who see an ad often check reviews or ask AI tools before buying, so consistent product details and real reviews help ads convert.
