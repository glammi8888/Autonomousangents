Claim and verify the profile, pick the most specific primary category, list every service, add real photos, keep hours current, ask happy customers for reviews and reply to them. Google says local results are based mainly on relevance, distance and prominence, and a complete profile helps with all three.

Set Up and Verify Your Profile

Search for your business name on Google Maps first. If a listing already exists, claim it instead of creating a second one, since duplicate listings confuse customers and split your reviews. If none exists, add your business from a Google account you will keep long term, ideally one the business owns rather than an employee's personal account.

Google then asks you to verify that you run the business. Depending on the business, that can be a video recording, a phone or email code or another method Google offers at the time. Your profile only shows your edits publicly once it's verified, so finish this step before anything else.

Get the Basics Exactly Right

Your name, address, phone number and website are the details customers act on and the details Google compares with the rest of the web:

  • Name: your real business name as it appears on your sign and website, with no added keywords or locations.
  • Address or service area: show your address if customers visit you. If you travel to customers, set a service area and hide the address.
  • Phone and website: a number that's answered and a link to the most relevant page, which is often your homepage or a booking page.
  • Hours: regular hours plus special hours for holidays, so nobody arrives to a closed door.

Use exactly the same name, address and phone number on your website, booking platforms, social profiles and directories.

Choose the Right Categories

Your primary category has a large effect on which searches you appear for. Choose the most specific one that describes what you are, like "Medical spa" instead of "Spa" or "Hair salon" instead of "Beauty salon" if hair is your focus. Then add a few secondary categories for other real parts of the business. Don't add categories for services you rarely offer.

List Your Services and Products

Add each service you offer with a short, plain description and a price or price range where it makes sense. Customers compare options before they call, and specific services also help Google and AI tools match you to specific searches, like "lash lift" or "engagement ring resizing." Retail businesses can add products with photos and prices.

Add Real Photos

Upload a clear logo and cover photo, then real photos of your storefront, interior, team and work. People want to see what they'll walk into and what results look like. Add new photos every month or two, and only post client photos with their permission. Skip stock images and heavy filters, since customers notice when the place doesn't match the pictures.

Earn and Answer Reviews

Reviews are one of the strongest signals for both customers and Google. Ask every happy customer, at the moment they're happiest, with a direct link to your review form. Ask them to mention the service they had, since detailed reviews are more useful to the next customer.

Reply to every review, positive or negative, within a few days. Thank people by name, and handle complaints calmly and briefly. Never buy reviews, offer rewards for them or post reviews for yourself: it's against Google's policies and can get reviews removed or the profile restricted. Healthcare businesses should also reply without confirming that the reviewer is a patient.

Keep the Profile Active

An active profile looks trustworthy. Post updates about new services, events or seasonal offers, answer customer messages quickly if you turn messaging on and check the profile monthly for suggested edits. Anyone can suggest changes to a listing, so review them and reject anything inaccurate.

Why Your Profile Matters for AI Search

When people ask ChatGPT, Google AI or Perplexity for a local recommendation, those answers often draw on the same information that powers local search: business listings, reviews and your website. A complete, consistent profile makes it easier for AI tools to describe you correctly. Mistakes That Can Get a Profile Suspended

  • Adding keywords or city names to your business name.
  • Using a virtual office or mailbox address you don't staff.
  • Creating more than one profile for the same business at the same address.
  • Posting fake reviews or rewarding customers for reviews.
  • Listing a business that doesn't meet customers in person or at its location.

Read Google's guidelines for representing your business before making big changes. A suspended profile can take weeks to restore.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is Google Business Profile optimization?

It's making your free Google listing complete, accurate and active: the right category, correct details, every service, real photos, steady reviews with replies and regular updates, so more nearby customers find and choose you.

How do I set up a Google Business Profile?

Search for your business on Google Maps and claim it if it exists, or add it from a Google account the business owns. Then verify it using the method Google offers and fill in every section.

Is a GMB profile the same as a Google Business Profile?

Yes. Google My Business was renamed Google Business Profile. GMB is the old name for the same free listing on Google Search and Maps.

How do I rank higher on Google Maps?

Google says local results depend on relevance, distance and prominence. You can't change distance, but a specific category, complete services, real photos, consistent details across the web and steady reviews help with the other two.

Does my Google Business Profile affect ChatGPT answers?

It can. AI tools that answer local questions often rely on business listings, reviews and websites, so an accurate, complete profile makes it more likely they describe your business correctly.
