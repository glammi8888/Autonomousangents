Most vision boards have a short life. You make one in January with a glue stick and a stack of magazines. It looks great. By March it's behind the door, and the life on it feels as far away as it did before. The board did its first job: it showed you what you want. Nobody told you about the second job. Here's how to make a vision board that does both.

Before You Cut Anything Out

Write first. Ten minutes, a pen, no images yet. Answer one question: what does an ordinary Tuesday look like a year from now? Where do you wake up? What are you working on? Who texts you? What's in your fridge?

That Tuesday is your brief. You're the art director now, and art directors don't grab every pretty picture. They pick the ones that fit the story. When you start collecting, every image has to earn its place against that Tuesday.

How to Make a Vision Board, Step by Step

1. Pick your format

Poster board and magazines if you like making things with your hands. A digital board if your ideas already live on your phone. Both work. The best format is the one you'll actually look at.

2. Collect more than you need

Go through your Pinterest saves and your screenshots. Most people already have a vision board. It's just scattered across Pinterest boards, screenshots and saved posts. Pull 30 to 50 images into one place before you choose anything.

3. Cut it down to 10 or 12

This is the step everyone skips. A board with 60 images is a mood. A board with 12 is a decision. Keep the ones that match your Tuesday and make your chest do something when you look at them.

4. Add the in-between

Look at what's left. If it's all outcomes (the apartment, the trip, the finished book), add a few images of the path: the desk where you write at 7am, the running shoes, the savings app, the class you keep meaning to book. In a study by Pham and Taylor, students who pictured the steps of studying did better on an exam than students who pictured getting the grade. Your board should show the steps too.

5. Write one line under each image

Start it with "This week I will". Keep it small enough to do in 20 minutes. "This week I will look at two apartments in that neighborhood." "This week I will write one page." Research on what psychologists call implementation intentions shows that deciding when and where you'll act makes you more likely to follow through.

6. Date it

Write today's date in a corner. In three months you'll want to know how far you've come.

Pro tip: leave one empty space on the board. When something on it starts happening, a reply, a yes, a first step, write it there. That space fills faster than you'd think, and seeing it fill is what keeps you going.

What to Put on It

  • The place you want to wake up in, down to the light and the sheets
  • The work you want to be known for
  • Your style, the version you'd wear if nobody was watching
  • One or two habits, shown as images, not words
  • The people you want around your table
  • A few words in your own voice, not someone else's quote

Skip anything you think you should want. A board full of other people's goals is the fastest way to stop looking at it.

Where to Keep It

Somewhere you'll see it without trying. Next to the mirror where you do your makeup. Above your desk. As your phone wallpaper. A board in a closet can't remind you of anything, and a Pinterest folder you never open is the digital version of the closet.

Four Reasons Boards Get Forgotten

  • It's only the finish line. Kappes and Oettingen found that indulging in positive fantasies about an ideal future can actually lower the energy you have to chase it. A board of only outcomes can feel like you've already arrived.
  • There's no next step. An image without an action stays a wish.
  • It never changes. You change. Update your board every few months, or it starts describing someone you used to be.
  • It's out of sight. See above. Move it.

You can't control the outcome. You can control what you do this week.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many pictures should a vision board have?

Around 10 to 12. Collect more, then cut down to the images that fit the life you're describing. Fewer images make clearer decisions.

What do you need to make a vision board?

A board or a digital canvas, images from magazines, photos or your Pinterest saves, a pen and about an hour. The most important part is ten minutes of writing before you choose any images.

Can I make a vision board on my phone?

Yes. Collect images from your saves into one album or app and set the finished board as your wallpaper, or use an app like ISSUE11 to turn your saves into magazine pages with a weekly step.

When is the best time to make a vision board?

Any time you're ready to decide what you want next. January is popular, but a board made in the middle of the year works the same way.

How often should I update my vision board?

Every two to three months, or when something on it happens or stops mattering to you.
