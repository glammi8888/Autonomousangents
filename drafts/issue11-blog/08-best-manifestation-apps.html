Start With One Question

What do you want the app to do for you on a regular Tuesday? Remind you of who you're becoming? Show you the life you want? Or ask what you're doing about it? Every app below answers one of those better than the rest.

If You Want Affirmations

ThinkUp lets you record affirmations in your own voice and play them back, which a lot of people find more convincing than reading someone else's words. It comes with a large library of affirmations by goal.

I Am puts short affirmations and quotes on your home screen through widgets and reminders at the times you choose. It's low effort by design: you see it whether or not you open the app.

Good for: mindset and repetition. Watch for: affirmations on their own don't tell you what to do next.

If You Want a Vision Board

Perfectly Happy is a dedicated vision board app with affirmations, a home-screen widget and morning and evening reminders. It's one of the most established apps in the category.

Canva and Milanote aren't manifestation apps, but many people use them to design a vision board: Canva for a polished, printable layout, Milanote for a looser collage of images and notes.

Pinterest is where most boards start. Nothing beats it for finding images. It was never built to help you act on them, which is why we wrote about turning your Pinterest saves into something you act on.

Good for: seeing the life you want. Watch for: a board you stop looking at after a month.

How to Choose

  1. Pick one job. Affirmations, a board or a weekly step. Apps that try to do everything usually do none of it well for you.
  2. Try it for two weeks. If you haven't opened it by day ten, it's not your app.
  3. Look for something that asks you a question. Research on goals keeps pointing to the same thing: specific plans for when and where you'll act make follow-through more likely than wishing alone.
