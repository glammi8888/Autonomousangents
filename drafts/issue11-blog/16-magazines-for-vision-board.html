The Best Magazines for Vision Boards, by Goal

Pick magazines for the life you want, not the ones lying around. A stack of gossip weeklies gives you a board full of other people's lives.

  • Your home: Architectural Digest, Domino, Elle Decor. Full-page rooms, kitchens you'd actually cook in, light you can almost feel.
  • Your style: Vogue, Harper's Bazaar, i-D. Big editorial photos crop well and make strong centerpieces.
  • Travel and slow living: Condé Nast Traveler, Travel + Leisure, Kinfolk.
  • Work and money: Fast Company, Inc. Look for desks, offices and people mid-pitch rather than headlines about billionaires.
  • Body and energy: Women's Health, Shape.
  • Food and hosting: Bon Appétit, Food & Wine. Great for a table full of friends.

Tip: cut words as well as pictures. A single headline like "The year she stopped waiting" can do more than five photos.

Where to Get Free Magazines for a Vision Board

  • Library book sales and discard carts. Many libraries sell or give away old issues. Never cut the copies on the shelves.
  • Buy Nothing groups. Post "looking for old magazines for a vision board" and you'll usually get a bag by the weekend.
  • Friends and family. Someone you know has a stack of Vogues they can't bring themselves to recycle.
  • Salons, offices and waiting rooms. Ask before taking anything. Old issues often get thrown out anyway.
  • Digital issues. With a library card, the Libby app lets you read many magazines for free. Screenshot what you love and print it.

How Many Magazines You Need

Five to ten magazines is enough for one board. Fewer than five and you end up settling for images that are almost right. More than fifteen and you'll spend the whole afternoon flipping instead of choosing. If you're hosting a vision board party, plan on about five per person and ask everyone to bring two.

How to Cut a Magazine for Your Board

  1. Flip first, cut later. Tear out every page that makes you feel something, without deciding why.
  2. Sort into piles by area of your life: home, work, body, love, style.
  3. Keep one image per goal on the board. The rest go in an envelope for later.
  4. Write one small move next to each image. "Linen sheets" becomes "wash and iron the good sheets this Sunday."

How to Make a Vision Board Without Magazines

You don't need magazines at all. Print your saved images, use your own photos or write words on index cards. If your saves already live on your phone, start with a Pinterest vision board and print only the ten that matter most.
