Why Journal Before the New Year?

Because most new year plans skip the looking-back part. If you don't notice what worked and what drained you this year, you tend to set the same goals and hit the same walls. Writing about it also helps: research on expressive writing, started by psychologist James Pennebaker, has linked putting thoughts and feelings on paper with benefits for mood and wellbeing. You don't need a special notebook. A notes app and one quiet evening are enough.

20 End of Year Reflection Questions

  1. What am I most proud of this year, even if nobody saw it?
  2. What surprised me about myself?
  3. What did I finally stop doing?
  4. Which three moments do I want to remember?
  5. What drained my energy the most?
  6. Who showed up for me, and did I thank them?
  7. What did I want but never started? Why?
  8. What was harder than I expected?
  9. What was easier than I expected?
  10. Where did my money go, and did it match what I care about?
  11. What did I learn the hard way?
  12. When did I feel most like myself?
  13. What habit helped me most?
  14. What am I still carrying that I can put down?
  15. What did I say yes to that I should have said no to?
  16. What did I save, screenshot or pin the most? What does that tell me?
  17. How did I take care of my body?
  18. What would I tell myself on January 1st of this year?
  19. Which version of me showed up most: the planner, the doer or the waiter?
  20. If this year had a title, what would it be?

How to Use These Prompts in One Evening

  1. Look back first (30 minutes). Answer five reflection questions. Pick the ones that make you pause.
  2. Take a break. Make tea. Let the year settle.
  3. Look ahead (30 minutes). Answer five new year prompts, always including 21, 28 and 29.
  4. Turn it into something you'll see. Put your answers on a new year vision board, one image per area with a small move under each.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are good new year journal prompts?

Good new year journal prompts mix looking back and looking ahead, like "What am I most proud of this year?" and "How do I want to feel next December?" Add one about obstacles, such as "What will probably get in my way, and what will I do when it does?"

What are good end of year reflection questions?

Try "What drained my energy the most?", "When did I feel most like myself?", "What did I finally stop doing?" and "If this year had a title, what would it be?" Pick five that make you pause and answer them honestly.

When should I do new year journaling?

Any time between mid-December and mid-January works. Many people use the quiet days between Christmas and New Year's, or the first weekend of January.

How long should I spend on new year journaling?

About an hour is enough: 30 minutes on reflection questions and 30 minutes on prompts for the year ahead, with a short break in between.

What do I do with my answers?

Turn them into something you'll see often, like a vision board or a word of the year, and pick one small move to make each week. Check back on your answers every few months.
