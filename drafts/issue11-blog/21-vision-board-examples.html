What Makes a Good Vision Board?

The boards that work aren't the prettiest. They're the ones you look at and act on. Research by psychologist Gabriele Oettingen found that only picturing the outcome can lower the energy you put into getting there, while pairing the picture with the obstacle and a plan works better. So every example below has a small move built in.

10 Vision Board Examples to Copy

1. The One-Word Board

Layout: your word of the year huge in the center, four to six images around it that show what that word looks like in real life.

Example: "Rest" in the middle, surrounded by a made bed with linen sheets, a phone charging in the kitchen, a slow Sunday breakfast and a book on a park bench.

Small move: one image gets one action each week. Need a word? See our word of the year ideas.

2. The Whole-Year Board

Layout: a grid of six boxes: home, work, money, body, love and you. One image and one line per box.

Example: a calm kitchen, a laptop in a café, a savings number written by hand, running shoes, a dinner table full of friends and a passport.

Small move: under each image, write the first step. Our new year vision board guide walks through it.

3. The Money Board

Layout: one clear number at the top, then images of what the money is for, not piles of cash.

Example: "$5,000 saved by June", a quiet apartment with your own furniture, a plane ticket, a laptop with your own business name on the screen.

Small move: automate one transfer this month and write the amount on the board.

4. The Career Board

Layout: the role or project in big words, with images of the day-to-day, not just the title.

Example: "Creative director", a desk with mood boards, a team meeting where you're talking, a business card, an outfit that feels like that job.

Small move: one conversation, application or portfolio piece per week.

5. The Self Love Board

Layout: softer, more words than images, with one photo of you.

Example: a recent photo you actually like, "I keep the promises I make to myself", a bath, a therapist's office door, flowers you bought yourself.

Small move: pair it with three believable new year affirmations.

6. The Home Board

Layout: one room per image, ordered from easiest to biggest change.

Example: a clear entryway, a reading corner, a plant on the windowsill, an open kitchen with friends cooking.

Small move: start with the cheapest change first, like clearing one shelf this Saturday.

7. The Travel Board

Layout: a map or list of places, with one image per trip and a date or month next to it.

Example: Lisbon in spring, a weekend cabin, a beach in Mexico, "first solo trip" in big letters.

Small move: open a travel savings account or block the dates in your calendar now.

8. The Health and Energy Board

Layout: how you want to feel, not how you want to look.

Example: a morning walk, a full night's sleep, a farmers market haul, a yoga mat, the word "Strong".

Small move: one habit at a time, like a ten-minute walk after lunch.

9. The Personal Rebrand Board

Layout: "before" words on the left, "becoming" images on the right.

Example: on the left, "waiting, people pleasing, someday". On the right, a new haircut, a bold outfit, a stage or a microphone, a calendar with your own plans in it.

Small move: pick one visible change this week. Our free Rebrand Kit has a plan for this.

10. The Digital Board

Layout: a phone wallpaper or a Pinterest vision board, with no more than nine images so it stays readable.

Example: a 3x3 grid on your lock screen with your word in the middle tile.

Small move: every time you unlock your phone, you see it. Every Sunday, pick one tile to act on.

Things to Put on a Vision Board

  • Images of ordinary days in the life you want, not only big moments.
  • One word or phrase for the year.
  • Numbers with a date, like a savings goal by June.
  • A photo of you, so the board is about your life, not someone else's.
  • Proof of something you already did that scared you.
  • One small move written under each image.

More ideas for each area of life are in our list of vision board ideas.

Common Vision Board Mistakes

  • Too many images. Past about nine, nothing stands out.
  • Only outcomes. A mansion with no first step is just a nice picture.
  • Someone else's life. If you picked it because it's popular on Pinterest, take it off.
  • Hiding it. A board in a drawer can't remind you of anything.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a good vision board look like?

A good vision board has one clear theme, five to nine images, a word or phrase for the year and a small action written next to each image. It hangs or lives somewhere you see every day.

What should I put on my vision board?

Put images of ordinary days in the life you want, one word for the year, numbers with dates, a photo of yourself and one small next step under each image.

What are the different types of vision boards?

Common types include a one-word board, a whole-year board with six life areas, money, career, home, travel, health, self love and personal rebrand boards, plus digital boards on your phone or Pinterest.

How many pictures should a vision board have?

Five to nine images is a good range. Fewer makes each one stand out, and you can always swap images out during the year.

Can a vision board be digital?

Yes. A phone wallpaper, a Pinterest board or an app works as well as paper, as long as you see it often and act on it.
