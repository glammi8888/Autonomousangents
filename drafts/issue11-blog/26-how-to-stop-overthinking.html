To stop overthinking, interrupt the loop and give your mind something concrete to do: write the thought down, name the one decision you actually face, take a small action or move your body for a few minutes. Below are nine practical ways to get out of your head, what to do when it happens at night and when overthinking is a sign to talk to a professional.

Why Your Brain Keeps Looping

Overthinking usually comes in two flavors: replaying the past ("why did I say that?") and rehearsing the future ("what if it goes wrong?"). Psychologists call the first one rumination. A well-known review by Susan Nolen-Hoeksema and colleagues found that rumination tends to keep people stuck in a low mood and makes problem solving harder, not easier. It feels like thinking it through. It mostly isn't. The goal isn't to stop thinking. It's to switch from looping to doing.

9 Ways to Stop Overthinking

  1. Write it down. Get the loop out of your head and onto paper. Thoughts look smaller in your handwriting.
  2. Ask "is this solvable today?" If yes, write the first step. If no, write "parked" next to it and come back to it at a set time.
  3. Give worry a time slot. Fifteen minutes in the late afternoon for worrying on purpose. When a worry shows up earlier, tell it to wait.
  4. Name the one decision. Overthinking often hides a single choice. Write it as one sentence: "I need to decide whether to take the job."
  5. Take the smallest possible action. Send one email, make one call, open the document. Action breaks loops faster than more thinking.
  6. Move for five minutes. A walk around the block, stretching or dancing in the kitchen. Your body can pull your mind out.
  7. Use your senses. Name five things you can see, four you can hear, three you can touch. It brings you back to the room.
  8. Talk to one person. Saying it out loud to someone you trust often shows you the thought is smaller than it felt.
  9. Turn down the inputs. Mute the group chat and close the tabs. Less noise means less fuel.

How to Stop Overthinking at Night

  • Do a brain dump before bed. Two minutes, everything on your mind, then close the notebook.
  • Write tomorrow's first step. Your brain relaxes when it knows there's a plan.
  • Keep your phone out of reach. Scrolling at midnight feeds the loop.
  • If you can't sleep after about 20 minutes, get up, do something calm in dim light and go back when you feel sleepy.

How to Stop Overthinking Decisions

  1. Set a deadline. "I'll decide by Friday at 5pm."
  2. Limit your options to two or three. More options, more looping.
  3. Ask what you'd tell a friend. You're usually wiser for other people.
  4. Pick the option you can undo. If both are reversible, choose and adjust later.
  5. Decide, then take one step. The step makes it real and quiets the "what ifs".

When to Get Support

These tips are for everyday overthinking. If your thoughts feel constant, keep you from sleeping or working, or come with panic, low mood or thoughts of harming yourself, please talk to a doctor, therapist or counselor. Therapies like cognitive behavioral therapy are designed for exactly this. In the US, you can call or text 988 any time to reach the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I stop overthinking everything?

Write the thought down, ask whether it's solvable today and take the smallest possible action if it is. Give worry a set time slot, move your body for a few minutes and cut down on inputs like constant notifications.

Why do I overthink so much?

Overthinking often comes from wanting certainty or control, especially during stress. Replaying the past or rehearsing the future can feel productive, but research on rumination shows it tends to keep people stuck rather than solve the problem.

How do I stop overthinking at night?

Do a short brain dump before bed, write tomorrow's first step, keep your phone out of reach and, if you can't sleep after about 20 minutes, get up and do something calm until you feel sleepy.

Is overthinking a sign of anxiety?

It can be. Everyone overthinks sometimes, but if it's constant, affects your sleep or daily life or comes with panic or low mood, it's worth talking to a doctor or therapist.

How do I stop overthinking decisions?

Set a deadline, limit your options to two or three, ask what you'd tell a friend and pick the option you can undo. Then take one small step to make the decision real.
