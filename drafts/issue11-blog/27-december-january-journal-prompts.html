Why a Prompt a Day Works

A blank page in a busy month is easy to skip. One question takes two minutes and gives you somewhere to start. Writing about your thoughts and feelings has been studied for decades, starting with psychologist James Pennebaker's work on expressive writing, and short, regular sessions are easier to keep than long ones. Miss a day? Skip it and do today's. The calendar is a guide, not a test.

31 Journal Prompts for December

Week 1: Slow down

  1. What does a good December feel like to me, in one sentence?
  2. What can I take off my list this month?
  3. Which tradition do I actually love, and which one am I keeping out of habit?
  4. Where in my day can I find ten quiet minutes?
  5. What would rest look like this weekend?
  6. Who do I want to see before the year ends?
  7. What's one cozy thing I can do tonight?

Week 2: Notice

  1. What made me smile today?
  2. Which song, smell or food feels like this season to me?
  3. What am I quietly proud of this month?
  4. Who helped me this year without being asked?
  5. What's something small I own that I'm grateful for?
  6. What did I learn from a hard moment this year?
  7. Where did I feel most at home this year?

Week 3: Give and receive

  1. What gift would mean the most to me, and could I give it to myself?
  2. Who would love a handwritten note from me?
  3. How do I like to receive care?
  4. What boundary will protect my energy during the holidays?
  5. Which conversation am I avoiding, and what's one kind first sentence?
  6. What do I want to say no to this season?
  7. What does generosity look like when money is tight?

Week 4: Close the year

  1. Which photo from this year tells the truest story?
  2. What am I ready to stop carrying into January?
  3. Who did I become this year?
  4. What was the best ordinary day I had this year?
  5. What surprised me most about this month?
  6. What would I like to forgive, in myself or someone else?
  7. What did I save or screenshot the most this year, and what does it say about what I want?
  8. What three words describe this year?
  9. What do I want to remember about this exact week?
  10. Write a short thank-you note to this year.

31 Journal Prompts for January

Week 1: Begin softly

  1. How do I want to feel when I wake up this year?
  2. What's one thing I'm curious about trying?
  3. What would make this week feel like a good start?
  4. What does "fresh start" mean to me, without changing everything?
  5. Which small habit would make the biggest difference?
  6. Where do I want my phone to live at night?
  7. What did I do well this week?

Week 2: Picture it

  1. Describe an ordinary Saturday in the life I want.
  2. What does my home look like when I'm doing well?
  3. What does a good money month look like for me?
  4. What kind of friend do I want to be this year?
  5. Which saved image best captures the life I'm going for?
  6. What would my most confident self wear tomorrow?
  7. What's one place I want to feel at home in this year?

Week 3: Make it real

  1. What's the smallest step toward the life I described this week?
  2. What usually stops me, and what will I do when it shows up?
  3. What can I prepare tonight that helps future me tomorrow?
  4. Who could I ask for help or advice?
  5. What does progress look like if it's not perfect?
  6. What did I do this week that the old me wouldn't have?
  7. What deserves a tiny celebration?

Week 4: Keep going

  1. Which new habit is sticking, and why?
  2. Which one isn't, and what would make it easier?
  3. What am I learning about myself this month?
  4. When did I feel most like myself this week?
  5. What can I let be "good enough"?
  6. Where do I need more rest?
  7. What's one kind thing I can say to myself when I slip?
  8. What do I want to carry into February?
  9. What surprised me about this month?
  10. Write a short letter to the person I'll be next December.

How to Keep Journaling Through the Holidays

  • Attach it to something you already do. Morning coffee, the train or right before bed.
  • Keep it to two minutes. One honest sentence counts.
  • Use your phone if paper won't happen. A notes app is still journaling.
  • Skip without guilt. Missed three days? Start with today's prompt.
  • Look back every Sunday. Five minutes rereading the week shows you patterns.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I journal about in December?

Focus on slowing down and closing the year: what you're grateful for, which traditions you love, the boundaries that protect your energy and what you're ready to leave behind before January.

What are good journal prompts for January?

Try "How do I want to feel when I wake up this year?", "What's the smallest step toward the life I want?" and "What usually stops me, and what will I do when it shows up?"

How do I journal every day during the holidays?

Keep it to two minutes, attach it to something you already do like your morning coffee and use one prompt a day. If you miss a day, skip it and do today's prompt.

Is January a good time to start journaling?

Yes. Many people start in January because it feels like a fresh start. Begin with one short prompt a day rather than long entries, so the habit is easy to keep.

Do I need a paper journal?

No. A notes app works too. The best journal is the one you'll actually open.
