10 Best Manifestation Books

1. The Secret by Rhonda Byrne

The book that made "law of attraction" a household phrase. It's easy to read and full of encouragement to focus on what you want. Take with a grain of salt: it leans hard on the idea that thoughts alone attract outcomes, which research doesn't support. Read it for the motivation, not the mechanics.

2. Manifest by Roxie Nafousi

A modern, step-by-step guide that ties manifesting to self-worth, inner work and action. A friendly first book if you want something structured and current. Best for: beginners who like clear steps.

3. You Are a Badass by Jen Sincero

Part pep talk, part manifesting guide, very funny. It pushes you to stop playing small and take action. Best for: people who need a confidence kick more than a theory.

4. Rethinking Positive Thinking by Gabriele Oettingen

Written by a psychology professor, this is the research-backed counterpoint. Her studies found that only daydreaming about a happy ending can drain motivation, and she offers a better method: picture the wish, then the obstacle, then the plan. Best for: skeptics and anyone whose vision boards never seem to turn into anything.

5. Atomic Habits by James Clear

Not a manifestation book, but the missing half of most of them: how to turn the person you want to be into small daily habits. Best for: people who are great at dreaming and stuck on follow-through.

6. The Universe Has Your Back by Gabrielle Bernstein

A spiritual take focused on trust, surrender and letting go of fear. Best for: readers who want a gentle, faith-centered approach and like daily practices.

7. Feeling Is the Secret by Neville Goddard

A short classic that many law of assumption fans start with. It focuses on imagining your wish as already fulfilled. Best for: people curious about where modern "assume it's done" ideas come from. Read our guide to the law of assumption for an honest take.

8. Think and Grow Rich by Napoleon Hill

One of the oldest success books, about desire, persistence and planning around money and career goals. Some ideas feel dated, but the focus on a definite goal and a written plan still holds up. Best for: money and career manifesting.

9. The Power of Your Subconscious Mind by Joseph Murphy

A classic about the stories you repeat to yourself and how they shape your choices. Best for: readers who like affirmations and want the old-school version.

10. The Artist's Way by Julia Cameron

A 12-week creative recovery course built around daily morning pages. Not labeled as manifesting, but many readers use it that way: writing every day makes it clear what you actually want. Best for: creatives and journalers.

How to Read a Manifestation Book So It Changes Something

  1. Read with a pen. Mark one idea per chapter you'll actually try.
  2. Turn one idea into a weekly action. "Visualize daily" becomes "five minutes picturing Monday's presentation going well, then prepare one slide."
  3. Keep what works, skip what doesn't. You don't have to accept a whole philosophy to use one good tool from it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the best book on manifestation?

For beginners, Manifest by Roxie Nafousi is a clear, modern start. The Secret is the most famous. If you want research, Rethinking Positive Thinking by Gabriele Oettingen is the strongest pick.

What manifestation book should I read first?

Start with Manifest by Roxie Nafousi or You Are a Badass by Jen Sincero, then read Rethinking Positive Thinking for a grounded, research-based balance.

Is The Secret worth reading?

It's worth reading for motivation and to understand why manifesting became popular, but its claim that thoughts alone attract outcomes isn't supported by research. Pair it with action.

Is there a scientific book about manifesting?

Rethinking Positive Thinking by psychologist Gabriele Oettingen is based on her research on wishes and goals, and shows why pairing a dream with obstacles and a plan works better than dreaming alone.

What is the Neville Goddard book about?

Feeling Is the Secret focuses on imagining your wish as already fulfilled, especially before sleep. It's the root of many modern law of assumption ideas.
