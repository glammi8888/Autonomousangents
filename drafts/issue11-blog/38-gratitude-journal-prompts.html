Why Gratitude Journaling Works

In a well-known study, psychologists Robert Emmons and Michael McCullough asked people to write down things they were grateful for each week. Compared with groups who wrote about hassles or neutral events, the gratitude group reported feeling better about their lives overall. The catch most people miss: writing the same three things every day gets stale fast. Rotating prompts keeps you looking, and looking is the part that changes how your day feels.

15 Daily Gratitude Prompts

  1. What made today easier than it could have been?
  2. What's one small thing I'd miss if it disappeared tomorrow?
  3. Which moment today would I happily replay?
  4. What did I eat, drink or smell today that I enjoyed?
  5. Who made me laugh this week?
  6. What's something in my home that quietly makes life better?
  7. What went right that I almost didn't notice?
  8. What did my body let me do today?
  9. What's a problem I don't have anymore?
  10. Which song, show or book am I glad exists right now?
  11. What's something I learned today, even something tiny?
  12. Where did I feel calm today?
  13. What did someone do for me without being asked?
  14. What's a comfort I usually take for granted?
  15. What would past me be amazed I have now?

10 Gratitude Prompts for Hard Days

  1. What's one thing that didn't go wrong today?
  2. Who could I call right now if I needed to?
  3. What got me through the worst part of today?
  4. What's a strength this hard day is showing me?
  5. What can I look forward to, even something small, this week?
  6. Which comfort is waiting for me at home tonight?
  7. Which hard time from my past am I glad is over?
  8. What is still steady while everything else feels shaky?
  9. What did I handle today that I couldn't have handled a year ago?
  10. What's one reason tomorrow might be a little better?

10 Prompts About the People in Your Life

  1. Who believes in me more than I believe in myself?
  2. Which friend always shows up, and how?
  3. What did a family member teach me that I still use?
  4. Who made a hard season easier, and have I told them?
  5. Which stranger's kindness do I still remember?
  6. Who makes me feel completely like myself?
  7. What's a habit of my partner or best friend I secretly love?
  8. Which teacher, boss or mentor changed my path?
  9. Who would I like to thank this week, and for what exactly?
  10. Who do I enjoy being around without needing to talk?

  2. Which decision from last year am I thankful for now?

  4. What's a skill I've built that makes life easier?

10 Thanksgiving Gratitude Questions

  1. What's my favorite Thanksgiving memory, and why does it stick?
  2. Which tradition would I keep forever?
  3. Who's missing from the table this year that I'm grateful I knew?
  4. What's a dish that tastes like home to me?
  5. What surprised me this year in a good way?
  6. What's something I have this year that I didn't have last November?
  7. Who would I like to sit next to at dinner, and what would I say to them?
  8. What's one thing I can give someone this week?
  9. Which small joy from this fall do I want to remember?
  10. What would I want everyone at the table to know I'm thankful for?

Tip: these work as table questions too. Write one per card and pass them around.

A 3-Minute Gratitude Routine

  1. Pick one prompt (30 seconds). Rotate through the sections so it stays fresh.
  2. Write three specific answers (2 minutes). "The barista remembered my order" beats "people."
  3. Choose one to act on (30 seconds). Thank the person, repeat the thing, savor it again tomorrow.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are good gratitude journal prompts?

Specific ones work best, like "What made today easier than it could have been?", "What's a problem I don't have anymore?" and "Who made a hard season easier, and have I told them?"

What should I write in a gratitude journal?

Answer one prompt with three specific details, then choose one to act on, like thanking someone. Specific beats general: name the exact moment, person or thing.

How often should I write in a gratitude journal?

A few minutes most days works well, but even once or twice a week helps. Rotating prompts keeps it from feeling repetitive.

How do I practice gratitude on a bad day?

Keep it small and honest: "What's one thing that didn't go wrong today?" or "Who could I call if I needed to?" Gratitude doesn't mean pretending the day was good.

What are good Thanksgiving gratitude questions?

Try "What's something I have this year that I didn't have last November?", "Which tradition would I keep forever?" and "What would I want everyone at the table to know I'm thankful for?"
