For beginners, the key is to go gently: one prompt, ten minutes, and permission to stop. Below is how to start, 40 shadow work prompts and how to know when it's time to talk to someone.

What Is Shadow Work?

The idea of the "shadow" comes from psychiatrist Carl Jung: the parts of ourselves we push out of sight because they feel unacceptable. Today, shadow work usually means noticing those parts with curiosity instead of shame. A strong reaction to someone else is often the starting clue. Writing helps because it slows the feeling down enough to look at it, and research on expressive writing suggests putting difficult feelings into words can be good for you when it's done gently.

How to Start Shadow Work Journaling

  1. Pick a calm moment. Not right after a fight or late at night when everything feels bigger.
  2. Choose one prompt. Start with triggers, the easiest way in.
  3. Write for ten minutes, then stop. Set a timer so it doesn't turn into a spiral.
  4. End with something kind. Write one sentence you'd say to a friend who wrote what you just wrote.
  5. Do something grounding after. A walk, a shower, a cup of tea.

10 Prompts About Triggers

  1. Who annoyed me this week, and what exactly did they do?
  2. What trait in other people bothers me more than it seems to bother anyone else?
  3. When did I last overreact, and what was I really afraid of?
  4. Whose success makes me uncomfortable, and why?
  5. What kind of criticism stings the most?
  6. What do I judge harshly in others that I might also do?
  7. Which comment from years ago do I still replay?
  8. What situation makes me want to leave the room?
  9. Where do I feel jealous, and what does that jealousy want?
  10. What would I never want anyone to say about me?

10 Prompts About Patterns

  1. Which argument do I keep having with different people?
  2. How do I usually react when I feel left out?
  3. What role did I play in my family growing up?
  4. Which feeling do I turn into a different feeling, like sadness into anger?
  5. What do I do when I feel out of control?
  6. Where do I sabotage things that are going well?
  7. What kind of person do I keep being drawn to, and why?
  8. Which promise to myself do I break most often?
  9. What do I reach for when I don't want to feel something?
  10. How do I act when I think I've disappointed someone?

10 Prompts About the Parts You Hide

  1. What part of me do I hide at work?
  2. Which emotion was I told as a child was "too much"?
  3. What do I secretly want that feels selfish to admit?
  4. What would I do if I stopped caring what people think?
  5. Which of my opinions do I keep quiet?
  6. What am I ashamed of that I've never written down?
  7. Which need do I act like I've outgrown?
  8. Which version of me do I only show to one person?
  9. What would my anger say if it could talk calmly?
  10. What part of my past do I edit out when I tell my story?

10 Prompts for Self-Acceptance

  1. What was the hidden part of me trying to protect me from?
  2. How has my "flaw" also helped me?
  3. What would I say to my younger self in that hard moment?
  4. What does the part of me I hide need from me now?
  5. Which trait could be a strength if I used it on purpose?
  6. What would it feel like to stop fighting this part of me?
  7. What's one way I can meet this need in a healthier way?
  8. What did I learn about myself today that I'm glad to know?
  9. How can I be a little kinder to myself this week?
  10. What is one small thing I'll do differently next time I'm triggered?

When to Get Support

Shadow work can stir up a lot. Stop and reach out to a therapist or counselor if a prompt brings up trauma, if you feel worse for days afterward, or if you notice thoughts of harming yourself. Journaling is a self-help tool, not a replacement for professional care, and there's no prize for doing the hard parts alone. Frequently Asked Questions

What is shadow work journaling?

It's writing about the parts of yourself you usually avoid, like jealousy, anger or old hurts, so you can understand them with curiosity instead of shame.

How do I start shadow work as a beginner?

Pick a calm moment, choose one prompt about a recent trigger, write for ten minutes, end with one kind sentence to yourself and do something grounding afterward.

Is shadow work safe?

Gentle shadow work journaling is fine for many people, but it can bring up painful memories. Stop if you feel overwhelmed and talk to a therapist or counselor, especially if trauma comes up.

How often should I do shadow work?

Once or twice a week in short sessions is plenty. Leave space between sessions to process.

What are examples of shadow work prompts?

Try "What trait in other people bothers me more than it seems to bother anyone else?", "Which argument do I keep having with different people?" and "What was the hidden part of me trying to protect me from?"
