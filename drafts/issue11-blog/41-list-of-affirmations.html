A list of affirmations is a set of short, positive sentences you repeat to steer your thoughts, like "I can be nervous and brave at the same time." The best list is one sorted by what you need right now, so you can pick two that feel true and use them daily. How to Use This List of Affirmations

Pick two lines that feel true or almost true, not twenty. Say them in the morning or write them somewhere you'll see them, and swap them when they stop landing. Research on self-affirmation suggests that reminding yourself of what you value can make stressful moments feel less threatening, which is why a short, believable line works better than a big claim you don't buy. Frequently Asked Questions

What are good affirmations to say every day?

Pick short lines you can believe, like "I am growing even when I can't see it", "I only have to handle this moment" and "Today is a fresh page." Two a day you mean beat a long list you rush through.

Do positive affirmations actually work?

They can help when they're believable and tied to what you value. Research on self-affirmation suggests it can make stressful situations feel less threatening, but affirmations support action rather than replace it.

What are some funny affirmations?

Try "I am thriving, mostly", "My to-do list fears me", "I attract parking spots" and "I am a work in progress with excellent snacks."

How many affirmations should I use at once?

Start with two. Repeat them daily for a week or two, then swap them when they stop feeling meaningful.

How do I write my own affirmation?

Use the present tense, keep it short and make it believable. If "I love my body" feels false, try "I'm grateful for what my body can do today" instead.
