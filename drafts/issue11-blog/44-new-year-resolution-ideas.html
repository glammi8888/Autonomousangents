How to Choose Resolutions You'll Keep

Resolutions get a bad reputation, but they work more often than people think. In one study, 46% of people who made resolutions were still succeeding six months later, compared with 4% of people who wanted to change but didn't make a resolution (Norcross, Mrykalo and Blagys, 2002). The ones that last tend to share three things:

  1. They're specific. "Walk 20 minutes after lunch" beats "get healthy".
  2. They're few. Pick three, not twelve.
  3. They're about identity. "I'm someone who reads" sticks better than "read more".

Pro tip: before you pick, write one sentence about who you want to be on December 31, 2027. Keep only the resolutions that person would actually do.

How to Keep Your New Year Resolutions

"New year, new me" works best when the new you shows up a little each day. Four things help most:

  • Make an if-then plan. "If it's 7 am, then I walk before I check my phone." Simple plans like this make people much more likely to follow through (Gollwitzer, 1999).
  • Start smaller than you think. Two pages, not twenty. One savings transfer, not a new budget.
  • See it every day. Put your resolutions on a new year vision board where you'll notice it.
  • Don't wait for January 1. Start now with a winter arc, so you arrive in the new year already in motion.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are good new year resolution ideas?

Good resolutions are small and specific, like walking 20 minutes a day, saving a set amount every payday, reading one book a month or calling a friend every week. Pick two or three that fit the person you want to become.

What are the most common new year resolutions?

The most common ones are exercising more, eating better, saving money, losing weight and spending less time on the phone.

How many new year resolutions should I make?

Two or three. Fewer resolutions are easier to remember and give each one a real chance.

How do you stick to new year resolutions?

Make them specific, start small, use an if-then plan such as "if it's 7 am, then I walk" and keep them where you see them every day.

What is a unique new year resolution?

Try a solo trip, a photo a day, a monthly "yes to something scary" or a letter to yourself to open next New Year's Eve.
