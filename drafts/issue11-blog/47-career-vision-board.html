What Is a Career Vision Board?

It's a vision board focused on your work: the job title you're aiming for, the business you want to start, the salary you want to earn, the skills you want to learn and how you want your workdays to feel. Unlike a general board, it's about one area of life, which makes it easier to turn into a plan.

Pro tip: put feelings on it, not just things. "Calm Monday mornings" or "a team that gets me" tell you more about the right next job than a corner office photo.

How to Make a Career Vision Board in 6 Steps

  1. Write your one-year sentence. "By next December, I'm..." Finish it honestly.
  2. Collect images. Save offices, outfits, cities, logos of companies you admire, quotes and numbers. Pinterest and your camera roll are good places to start.
  3. Edit to 8 to 12. Keep only what makes you lean in.
  4. Add words. A target title, a salary number, a skill, a word for the year.
  5. Write one step under each image. "Update LinkedIn headline", "Ask Sam for coffee", "Finish the course module."
  6. Put it where you work. Your desk, your laptop wallpaper or your phone's lock screen.

30 Career Vision Board Ideas

If You're Starting Out

  • The title of the first job you want.
  • A photo of the city you want to work in.
  • Your dream first-week outfit.
  • A skill you'll learn this year, like Excel, design or public speaking.
  • A mentor you want to meet.
  • Your first salary goal, written as a number.
  • A desk setup you love.
  • A quote that makes you brave.
  • A logo of a company you'd love to work for.
  • "First paycheck" savings goal.

If You're Growing

  • The next title up, written in bold.
  • A raise percentage you'll ask for.
  • A conference you want to speak at.
  • A certification or course to finish.
  • An image that feels like leading a team.
  • A "new business cards" mockup.
  • A portfolio piece you want to make.
  • A boundary: "Laptop closed by 7".
  • A trip you'll pay for with your bonus.
  • A photo of you at your most confident.

If You're Changing or Starting Something

  • Your future business name.
  • A storefront, studio or home office you picture.
  • Your first client or first sale, written as a date.
  • Monthly income you want from your own work.
  • A screenshot of a website style you love.
  • The words "I quit" (or "I launched").
  • A morning routine that feels like freedom.
  • One person you'll tell about your plan.
  • A book on your new field.
  • An image of what you'll do with the extra time.

Career Vision Board Examples

  • The promotion board: next title in the middle, a salary number, a leadership quote and three skills, each with one action.
  • The career change board: new field images, one course, two people to talk to and a date to apply by.
  • The side business board: logo ideas, first product, a sales goal and a "launch day" photo.
  • The work-life board: a calm desk, a walk at lunch, a closed laptop at 6 and a vacation you'll actually take.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a career vision board?

A career vision board is a collage of images and words showing the work life you want, like a role, income, skills and how your days feel, with small next steps that move you toward it.

What should I put on a career vision board?

Your target title, a salary number, skills to learn, companies or cities you admire, a mentor you want to meet and images of how you want your workdays to feel.

How do I make a career vision board?

Write a one-year sentence, collect images, edit to 8 to 12, add words and numbers, write one small step under each image and keep it where you work.

Can I make a digital career vision board?

Yes. Use Canva, Pinterest or the Freeform app on iPhone, then set it as your laptop or phone wallpaper so you see it every workday.

Do career vision boards work?

A board can't get you a job, but it can keep your goal in front of you. It works best with a clear next step under each image and a monthly check-in.
