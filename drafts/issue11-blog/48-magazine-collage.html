A magazine collage is a picture made by cutting images, words and textures out of magazines and layering them into one composition. You can make one in an evening with scissors, a glue stick and a stack of old magazines, and it works as art, a journal page or a vision board. Here's how to make a magazine collage step by step, the layout tricks that make it look intentional, and 20 ideas to try.

What You Need

  • Magazines: 5 to 10, ideally a mix of fashion, interiors, travel and food for variety. Our guide to the best magazines for collages and vision boards lists which ones have the best images and where to get them free.
  • A base: heavy paper, card stock, a journal page or a canvas board.
  • Sharp scissors and a craft knife for clean edges.
  • Glue: a glue stick for paper, matte gel medium if you want a smooth, sealed finish.
  • Optional: washi tape, a fine black pen and a ruler.

How to Make a Magazine Collage in 7 Steps

  1. Pick a theme or a feeling. "Slow Sundays", "my next apartment", "confidence". A theme stops the collage from turning into a random pile.
  2. Flip and tear fast. Go through the magazines quickly and tear out anything that pulls at you. Don't judge yet.
  3. Cut with intent. Trim the pieces you'll actually use. Cut around shapes for a clean look, tear edges for a softer one.
  4. Choose an anchor. One big image that carries the mood. Everything else supports it.
  5. Arrange before you glue. Lay everything out, move it around, take a photo of the version you like.
  6. Glue from the back layer forward. Backgrounds and textures first, people and words last.
  7. Add words. Cut single words or letters from headlines, or write your own in pen.

Pro tip: leave some empty space. Collages that cover every inch look busy; a little breathing room makes the anchor image stand out.

Layout Tricks That Make It Look Intentional

  • Stick to 2 or 3 colors. Pull pieces that share a palette, even if the subjects are different.
  • Mix scales. One huge face next to tiny objects creates drama.
  • Repeat a shape. Circles, stripes or hands repeated 3 times tie the page together.
  • Use texture as background. Close-ups of fabric, water, marble or sky make great backgrounds.
  • Let pieces overlap. Overlaps look designed; gaps look like a scrapbook.

20 Magazine Collage Ideas

About You

  • Your dream morning, from the coffee cup to the view.
  • Your style for the next season.
  • A self-portrait made only of things you love.
  • Your word of the year in cut-out letters.
  • Everything that makes you feel calm.

About Your Future

  • A new year vision board collage.
  • Your next home, room by room.
  • The trips you want to take this year.
  • A career collage of the work life you want.
  • Your future self's weekend.

Just for Art

  • One color only: everything red, or everything blue.
  • Eyes only.
  • Hands only.
  • A city made from food photos.
  • Portraits mashed together into one new face.

For Your Journal

  • A monthly cover page.
  • A mood page for how this week felt.
  • A gratitude page with images of small good things.
  • A page for one memory you want to keep.
  • Quotes cut from headlines that sound like advice. Pair with our vision board quotes.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a magazine collage?

A magazine collage is a composition made by cutting images, words and textures out of magazines and gluing them in layers onto paper, a journal page or a canvas.

How do you make a magazine collage?

Pick a theme, tear out images that fit it, cut them cleanly, choose one anchor image, arrange everything before gluing, glue from the back layer forward and finish with cut-out words.

What glue is best for magazine collage?

A glue stick works for most paper collages and won't wrinkle thin magazine pages. Use matte gel medium if you want a smooth, sealed finish or you're working on canvas.

How do I make my collage look good?

Limit yourself to 2 or 3 colors, mix big and small images, let pieces overlap and leave some empty space around your main image.

Can you make a magazine collage digitally?

Yes. Use Canva, Pinterest's collage tools or the free Freeform app on iPhone to cut out, layer and arrange saved images and add text.
