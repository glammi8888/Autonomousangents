Why Make a Personal Magazine?

Because a vision board is a wall of pictures you stop seeing after a week, and a magazine is something you open. A cover, a story and pages give your ideas an editor's eye. It turns "things I like" into "the life I'm building", and it's a lot more fun to flip through on a Sunday than a folder of screenshots.

Personal Magazine Tutorial: 8 Steps

  1. Name your magazine. Your name, a nickname or a word for the year. This becomes your masthead.
  2. Pick the issue's theme. "The 2027 Issue", "The Reset Issue", "The New City Issue". One theme keeps it focused.
  3. Gather your images. Your own photos, Pinterest saves, screenshots and magazine cut-outs. Aim for 30 to 50 and edit down later.
  4. Plan the pages. A typical issue: cover, editor's letter, contents, 4 to 6 features, a closing page. Sketch the order on one sheet of paper.
  5. Design the cover. One strong image, your masthead at the top and 3 or 4 cover lines, like "How I'm Saving for Paris" or "My Winter Uniform".
  6. Write the editor's letter. Half a page to yourself about what this issue is about and why now.
  7. Lay out the features. One idea per spread: an image, a headline and a few lines. Leave white space.
  8. Make it real. Print it, bind it with a stapler or ribbon, or keep it digital on your phone.

Pro tip: end every feature with one small, doable step. "Book the haircut", "open the savings account". That's the difference between a pretty magazine and one that changes something.

12 Page Ideas for Your Magazine

  • The cover story: who you're becoming this year.
  • Editor's letter: a note to yourself.
  • Style file: your next season's outfits.
  • At home: the room you want to wake up in.
  • Money page: one goal and the first step.
  • Travel: the trip you'll actually take.
  • Beauty: your routine, simplified.
  • Career: the work life you want. Our career vision board ideas work as a starting point.
  • Words to live by: 5 quotes or affirmations.
  • Reading list: 3 books for the issue.
  • Little luxuries: the small things that make a day good.
  • Back page: one question for next issue's you.

A Personalized Magazine as a Gift

A personal magazine also makes a thoughtful gift for a birthday, an anniversary or a graduation. The steps are the same, with the person as the cover star:

  • Collect photos from friends and family, plus a few of their favorite things.
  • Write cover lines only they would get: inside jokes, nicknames, big moments.
  • Add a "letters" page where people write a few lines each.
  • Design it in a free tool like Canva, then order a printed copy from a print service. Most print services let you order a single magazine.
  • Give yourself at least 2 weeks for printing and shipping.
