New Year Intentions vs Resolutions

A resolution is a rule: "lose 10 pounds", "stop spending on takeout". Miss it once in February and the whole thing feels broken. An intention is a direction: "I want to feel strong in my body", "I want money to feel calm". You can't fail a direction. You just steer back.

That framing matters. In a 2020 study of more than 1,000 people's New Year's resolutions, goals phrased as something to move toward ("start", "add") were kept more often than goals phrased as something to avoid ("quit", "stop"): about 59% vs 47% after a year (Oscarsson et al., PLOS ONE). Intentions are toward-goals by nature.

You don't have to pick a side. Plenty of people set one intention and then hang a couple of resolutions underneath it as the steps.

How to Set Intentions for the New Year in 5 Steps

  1. Look back for ten minutes. What felt good this year? What drained you? Two lists, no essays. If you want more prompts, our new year journal prompts cover this.
  2. Pick one or two areas. Not seven. Home, money, body, work, love, friends, creativity: choose the one that kept coming up on your "drained" list.
  3. Write it as now, not someday. "I'm someone who cooks on weeknights" beats "I will try to cook more". Present tense, your words, one line.
  4. Attach one small move. The tiniest action that proves the intention is real this week. Psychologists call the "when X, I'll do Y" version an implementation intention, and it reliably helps people follow through (Gollwitzer, 1999). Example: "When I get home on Tuesday, I'll put the phone in the kitchen."
  5. Put it where you'll see it. Lock screen, mirror, the first page of your journal or your new year vision board. An intention you can't see is a wish.

Pro tip: if one word sums it up, that's your word of the year. The intention is the sentence, the word is the headline.

30 New Year Intention Examples

Steal the wording, change it until it sounds like you. Each one comes with a first move you could do this week.

For You

  • I keep promises to myself. First move: pick one tiny promise (water before coffee) and keep it for 7 days.
  • I take up space. First move: say your opinion first in one meeting or group chat.
  • I rest without earning it. First move: block one empty Sunday afternoon and tell nobody.
  • I'm gentle with myself on bad days. First move: write the sentence you'd text a friend, then send it to yourself.
  • I choose things I actually like. First move: unfollow 10 accounts that make you feel behind.
  • I live like the year is already good. First move: use the nice candle, the good plates, the perfume you're saving.

For Money

  • Money feels calm, not scary. First move: open your banking app and look at the real number for 2 minutes.
  • I spend on what I love and cut what I don't. First move: cancel one subscription you forgot you had.
  • I'm building a cushion. First move: set up an automatic transfer, even $10 a week.
  • I know my worth at work. First move: write down three wins from this year before your next review.

For Your Body and Energy

  • I move because it feels good. First move: a 15-minute walk with a podcast, no tracking.
  • I go to bed on purpose. First move: an alarm at 10:15 pm that just says "wind down".
  • I eat like someone I'm taking care of. First move: plan two dinners on Sunday.
  • I feel strong. First move: book one class you've been curious about. Our winter arc plan has a gentle version.
  • My phone is a tool, not a home. First move: move social apps off your home screen.

For Home

  • My home feels like a soft place to land. First move: clear one surface and keep it clear.
  • I host more. First move: put a casual dinner with two friends on the calendar for January.
  • I live with less stuff. First move: one bag out the door every Saturday in January.

For Work and Creativity

  • I make things again. First move: 20 minutes on Thursday evening for the thing you used to love.
  • I'm moving toward work that fits me. First move: message one person who has the job you're curious about.
  • I finish what I start. First move: pick one half-done project and define what "done" means.
  • I learn something just for fun. First move: sign up for one free class or library workshop.
  • I protect my mornings. First move: no email until after breakfast, three days a week.

For Love and Friendship

  • I show up for my people. First move: a recurring monthly call with your closest friend.
  • I let in people who are good for me. First move: say yes to one invitation you'd usually skip.
  • I say what I need. First move: practice one clear ask, like "Can you take Tuesdays?"
  • I'm open to love. First move: one new place a month where you might meet someone.
  • I leave room for quiet. First move: one night a week with no plans on purpose.
  • I forgive faster. First move: write the grudge down, then decide if it still deserves space.
  • I celebrate other people loudly. First move: send three "so proud of you" texts this week.

How to Keep Your Intentions Past January

Most intentions don't fail. They just get forgotten around week three, when real life is louder than the notebook. A few things help:

  • Keep it to one or two. You'll remember two. You won't remember nine.
  • Check in monthly, not daily. First Sunday of the month: does this still feel true? What's the next small move?
  • Pair it with a phrase you say out loud. A matching new year affirmation keeps it in your head.
  • Swap the move, keep the intention. If walks aren't happening, try a dance class. The direction stays, the step changes.
  • Make it something you want to look at. A list in a notes app is easy to ignore. Pictures of the life you want are harder to scroll past.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are new year intentions?

New year intentions are short statements about how you want to live and feel in the coming year, like "my mornings feel calm". Unlike resolutions, they describe a direction rather than a target you pass or fail.

How do you set intentions for the new year?

Look back at what felt good and what drained you, pick one or two areas of life, write each intention in the present tense, attach one small action you can do this week and put it somewhere you'll see daily.

What are some good new year intention examples?

"I keep promises to myself", "money feels calm, not scary", "I move because it feels good", "my home feels like a soft place to land" and "I show up for my people" are good examples, each paired with one small first move.

Are intentions better than resolutions?

Neither is better for everyone. Intentions are easier to keep through a bad week because there's no streak to break, and research on New Year's goals found that goals framed as moving toward something were kept more often than goals about quitting something.

How many intentions should I set for the new year?

One or two. You'll remember them, you'll see them often and each can have its own small weekly action. More than three usually means none get attention.
