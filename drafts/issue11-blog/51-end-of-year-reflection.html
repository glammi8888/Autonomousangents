An end of year reflection is an hour you set aside to look back at the year honestly: what happened, what you want to keep, what you're done with and what it taught you. The easiest way to do it is in four rounds of questions, then one page that sums the year up like the cover of a magazine. Below are 30 end of year reflection questions in that order, a best-of list, an in and out list for next year and a template you can copy.

Before You Start (5 Minutes)

Open your camera roll and scroll back to January. That's it. Your photos remember the year better than you do, and ten minutes of scrolling will bring back things you forgot happened. Then grab a notebook or a notes app, something to drink and a timer. Give each round about 12 minutes. You don't need full sentences.

Writing it down is worth the effort. Studies on expressive writing found that putting experiences into words helps people make sense of them and was linked to better wellbeing (Pennebaker and Seagal, 1999).

Round 1: The Highlights Reel

Start with the good stuff. It warms you up and it's the part people skip.

  1. What were the 5 best days of the year? What made them good?
  2. Best meal you ate. Where were you and who was there?
  3. The song you played the most.
  4. The best thing you bought, and the best thing that was free.
  5. A conversation you keep thinking about.
  6. A place you went for the first time.
  7. Something you made, fixed or finished.
  8. A moment you laughed so hard it hurt.

Gratitude research suggests that listing good things on a regular basis is linked to feeling better about life overall (Emmons and McCullough, 2003), so don't rush this round.

Round 2: The Honest Part

Now the year as it actually was, without the filter.

  1. What took more out of you than it should have?
  2. When did you go quiet when you wanted to speak up?
  3. What did you keep meaning to do and never touched? Do you still want it?
  4. Which week would you happily never repeat? What got you through it?
  5. What did you spend money on that you don't remember?
  6. Who did you lose touch with, on purpose or by accident?
  7. What were you afraid of this year that never happened?

Be kind here. You're reviewing the year, not grading yourself. If a question brings up something heavy, it's okay to skip it, and talking it through with someone you trust or a therapist can help more than a notebook.

Round 3: Keep, Change, Let Go

This round turns the looking back into decisions.

  1. Which habit from this year do you want to keep exactly as it is?
  2. Which one do you want to keep but make smaller?
  3. What is one thing you'll stop doing in January?
  4. Which person do you want to see more of next year?
  5. What part of your normal week do you actually love?
  6. What would make your home feel better by spring?
  7. What's one belief about yourself you'd like to leave in this year?
  8. What's the smallest change that would make next December feel different?

Round 4: Name the Year

Magazines sum up a year with a cover. Do the same for yours.

  1. If this year were a magazine issue, what would it be called? ("The Moving Issue", "The Survival Issue", "The Yes Issue")
  2. Which photo from your camera roll would be the cover?
  3. Write three cover lines, like "How I Finally Learned to Cook" or "Leaving the Job, Keeping the Friends".
  4. What's the headline you'd want on next year's cover?
  5. What one word do you want next year to feel like? (Our word of the year ideas can help.)
  6. What's one small move you can make in the first week of January toward that headline?
  7. Write a two-line note to yourself to open next December.

Pro tip: Question 29 is the one that matters most. A reflection that ends without a next move is just nostalgia.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an end of year reflection?

An end of year reflection is time you set aside in December to look back at the year: the highlights, the hard parts, what you want to keep or change and what you learned, usually by answering a set of questions in writing.

What are good end of year reflection questions?

Good ones cover four areas: highlights ("What were the 5 best days of the year?"), honesty ("What took more out of you than it should have?"), decisions ("What is one thing you'll stop doing in January?") and the year ahead ("What's one small move you can make in the first week of January?").

How do you do an end of year reflection?

Scroll your camera roll back to January, then spend about 12 minutes on each of four rounds: highlights, the honest part, keep/change/let go and naming the year. Finish with an in and out list and one small move for the first week of January.

When should you do an end of year reflection?

Any time in the last two weeks of December works. Many people do it between Christmas and New Year's Eve, when the calendar is quieter. One focused hour is enough.

Is there an end of year reflection template?

Yes. A simple one has ten lines: the year's title, cover photo, best 5 days, hardest stretch, keeping, changing, letting go, your in and out list, next year's headline and your first small move.
