Part 2 · 30 ChatGPT prompts

30 ChatGPT prompts to become your own dream girl.

Before you start: give ChatGPT something real (your schedule, your budget, what you love). Skip account numbers and anything private. If an answer feels generic, say: “Interview me. Ask one question at a time.”
Physical · Looking hotter, feeling better
01Future me has energy for my life.“Interview me, one question at a time, about my energy, sleep and movement right now. Then help me pick three priorities for 2027. Don’t assume weight loss is the goal.”Goals about how you want to feel last longer than goals about a number.
02A workout I actually like.“Ask what I’ve enjoyed before, what puts me off, and whether I like company, music or alone time. Suggest three options and a two-week trial before I commit.”Testing before committing takes away the pressure to get it perfect.
03My 5-minute glow up morning.“Here’s my normal morning: [paste]. Attach one small habit to something I already do every day. Give me a 2-minute version for bad days.”Habit stacking and tiny habits: an old habit becomes the reminder, and tiny means it doesn’t need motivation.
04Walk in like the cover star.“Ask me about three moments I want to feel confident in. For each, help me pick one thing I control (outfit, pace, my first sentence) and a low-pressure way to practice.”Small, rehearsed actions build self-efficacy: the belief that you can do it.
05Make taking care of me easier.“Ask about one habit I want and what makes it annoying to do now. Suggest one change to my space so it takes less effort. I’ll test it this week.”Changing your surroundings works better than relying on willpower.

Money · Business popping OFF
06What does “rich af” mean for me?“Ask what I earn now, what I want to earn in 2027, and what that money would make possible. Rough numbers are fine. Help me find a few realistic routes and separate facts from guesses.”A specific number makes a goal concrete, and concrete goals get more effort.
07See what I bring to the table.“Interview me about problems I solve and what people ask me for help with. If I downplay something, ask for the real example. Turn it into a skills list with proof.”Writing down past wins builds belief for the next one.
08Ask for more, no 3-paragraph apology.“Help me prepare to ask for a raise, a higher rate or a new offer. Draft my opening in my voice, then role-play it with me one reply at a time.”Rehearsing a hard conversation makes the real one easier to start.
09Give my business idea a real chance.“Use the WOOP method with me: my Wish, the best Outcome, my main inner Obstacle, then a Plan. End with a small, cheap test, a spending limit and a decision date.”WOOP (mental contrasting) beats positive thinking alone: you dream, then plan for what gets in the way.
10If it doesn’t happen, what stopped it?“Imagine it’s June 2027 and I gave up on [goal]. Ask me what went wrong, then help me plan around it now.”A pre-mortem spots problems before they happen.

Love · My people, my circle
11What my kind of love looks like.“Ask about affection, communication, independence and how we handle disagreements. Help me sort needs, nice-to-haves and what I’m still figuring out. Include what I want to give, too.”Knowing your values makes the big choices easier.
12Friends we actually make time for.“Ask who I want to see more and what gets in the way. Help me write one if-then plan, like: “If it’s the first Sunday of the month, then I text [name] a plan.””Implementation intentions: if-then plans make follow-through much more likely.
13Meet more of my people.“Ask about my interests and places I already like. Suggest three settings where I’d see the same people often, plus a one-month experiment with easy conversation starters.”Seeing the same faces again and again is one of the main ways friendships start.
14Say no, kindly.“I’m too grown to keep explaining myself. Help me write a short, kind way to set a boundary with [person]. Then play them, and give me feedback on how clear I was.”Ready-made words lower the cost of a boundary in the moment.
15My inner critic is loud today.“Here’s what it says: [paste]. Ask me to check the evidence: what’s true, what’s old, and what a friend would say.”Questioning a thought (from CBT) loosens its grip.

Fashion · Dress for the life you want
16My three style words.“Interview me about outfits I love, pins I save and how I want to feel getting dressed. Help me pick three style words, then test them on a workday, a weekend and a night out.”Enclothed cognition: what you wear can shift how you think and act.
17Shop my closet first.“Help me make new outfits from what I already own: [describe or share photos]. Start with my favorite pieces. Only list real gaps at the end.”Starting from what you love builds on what already works.
18A signature look that feels like me.“Using my favorite colors, shapes and pieces, give me three outfit formulas I’d happily wear on repeat, for different weather and plans.”Identity-based habits: every outfit is a small vote for who you’re becoming.
19A wish list with standards.“Ask about my budget, my gaps and purchases I loved or regretted. Help me set rules an item has to pass, and a 48-hour pause for anything I’m unsure about.”A short pause between wanting and buying helps you pick what you’ll love.
20Getting dressed is the easy part.“Ask which moments make me feel like I have nothing to wear. Build outfit formulas for them from my closet, shoes and accessories included.”Fewer small decisions in the morning leave room for the big ones.

Beauty · Glowing up
21My version of polished.“Ask what makes me feel put together, what I’d happily skip, and how much time and money I want to spend. Build an everyday routine and a going-out version from what I already use.”Your own definition of a goal is the one you’ll keep.
22Edit my beauty routine.“Here’s what I use: [list]. Sort it into love it, not sure, and could simplify. Don’t diagnose skin or hair concerns. Give me questions to ask a professional instead.”A simpler routine is an easier one to keep.
23Try something new with my look.“I’m curious about [hair, makeup, color]. Suggest three ways to try it, starting with the easiest to undo.”Small, reversible experiments make change feel safe.
24Make getting ready fun again.“Ask which parts of getting ready feel rushed. Pair my routine with something I love, like a playlist or podcast, and give me a 5-minute version for busy days.”Temptation bundling: the routine comes with a reward built in.
25What my glow up really costs.“Here are the treatments and products I’m considering: [list]. Map the full yearly cost, upkeep included, and help me fit what I’ll enjoy most into a budget I choose.”Seeing the full cost up front helps you spend on what you’ll love most.

Part 2 · 30 ChatGPT prompts
Personal finances · $20,000 saved
26Let’s have a money date.“Using rough totals (no account numbers), summarize what comes in, what goes out and what we still need to check. Give me one task for this week and a monthly money-date checklist.”Small, regular check-ins make money feel calmer than avoiding it.
27A budget with a life in it.“Here’s my real spending: [paste]. Build a plan that covers the bills and makes room for fun. Ask what made past budgets hard to follow.”A plan that includes fun is a plan you’ll stick to.
28What makes me hit “buy”?“Ask me about three recent purchases: what was happening before, what I hoped to feel, and how I felt after. No judging. Help me add a pause only where I want one.”Noticing the moment before a habit is the first step to changing it.
29$20,000 saved: what’s the plan?“Break my savings goal of [$ amount] by [date] into a weekly number. Show me three places it could come from in this budget: [paste], with the tradeoffs. No promises.”Breaking a big goal into small steps makes progress visible, and progress keeps you going.
30Put my 2027 on one page.“Bring my plans for body, money, love, style, beauty and savings into one first-month plan with one main focus. Then write five cover lines for my 2027 issue, as if I’m on the cover of my own magazine.”Seeing yourself as the main story makes the plan feel like yours.
Before you close the chat: “Give me a short recap in my own words: what I learned, what I’m choosing, and what I’ll try next.”
