## 1 · The angle in 2 lines
**AI helps you see it. Your Issue and small moves help you live it.**
Use ChatGPT (or any AI) to put words and pictures to a dream you've only felt, then take the picture somewhere: a page in your Issue and one next step this week.

**How it ties to ISSUE11**
- **SEE** (DESIRE → IMAGINE): the prompts turn a vague wish into a scene, a letter, an editorial photo. This is the free, shareable part.
- **LIVE** (EMBODY → BECOME): every prompt set ends on a real-life move or a proof note. That's ISSUE11's job: the magazine makes the future visible, small moves make it real (beliefs 1, 3, 6).
- **Guardrails:** ISSUE11 stays magazine-first. The content teaches people to use whatever AI they already have; it never pitches ISSUE11 as a chatbot, a coach or a productivity app. The app's own AI (the Editor) stays invisible in this content. CTA = the magazine (small ISSUE11 mark + issue11.app), never "chat with our AI".
- **What we never say:** "manifest faster", "guaranteed", "AI will make it happen", "become her", or anything that mocks people who believe. No outcome promises: a prompt can't deliver a job or a partner, a next step can move you toward one.

## 2 · 10 copy-paste prompts (free to use)
Written to paste straight into ChatGPT. Square brackets = fill in your own words. Each one is mapped to a belief from POINT-OF-VIEW.md so the series teaches the brand's stance without saying it out loud.

**1 · A day in my dream life** (belief 3: the board is step one)
> Here's what I want my life to look like in 3 years: [paste your list: work, home, money, love, body, friends, how you spend a Sunday]. Write one ordinary Tuesday in that life, from waking up to going to bed, talking to me as "you". Keep it specific and real: what I eat, who texts me, what I'm working on at 11am, what my place looks like. Only use luxury details I actually listed.

**2 · Rehearse the process** (belief 2: the process is yours)
> I want [goal]. Skip the moment I get it. Walk me through the first 10 minutes of working toward it on a normal weekday after work, when I'm tired. What do I open, what do I write, who do I message? Keep it under 150 words so I can picture it with my eyes closed.

**3 · A letter from future me** (belief 5: become YOU)
> Using everything I told you above, write a short letter from me, 3 years from now, to me today. It should sound like me on a good day: same words I use, same humour. No guru talk and no "you've got this". Mention one hard thing I got through and one small habit that helped.

**4 · The obstacle check** (belief 1: participation; based on mental contrasting research)
> Help me think through one wish. Ask me one question at a time and wait for my answer before the next: 1) what I want in the next 3 months, 2) the best thing about having it, 3) what inside me usually gets in the way, 4) an "if [that happens], then I will [do this]" plan. At the end, give me my answers back as 4 short lines I can save.

**5 · My next 5 minutes** (belief 3: a dream needs a next step)
> Here's my dream: [paste]. Give me one thing I can do in 5 minutes today and one thing I can do this week. Free, doable on my phone, and nothing that needs me to feel motivated first.

**6 · The editorial vision board photo** (image prompt; belief 3)
> Editorial fashion photograph for a magazine feature about my life in 3 years: [one scene, e.g. "my own design studio on a Saturday morning", "reading contracts on my balcony in Lisbon"]. A woman in her [30s], [your hair, skin tone, style], wearing [outfit]. Natural window light, shot on 35mm film, candid, not looking at the camera, muted colours, high-end magazine styling, real textures. No text, no logos, no brand names.

**7 · Cover lines for my next year** (the magazine as hero)
> Write 6 magazine cover lines for an issue about my next year, using my own words from above. Make them sound like a real fashion magazine cover: specific, a little cheeky, short. One should be about something I'm scared to try.

**8 · When it didn't happen** (belief 4: a setback is not a verdict)
> This month didn't go the way I pictured: [what happened]. Help me sort it into three lists: what's worth keeping, what to adjust, what I can pause or let go. Leave out any "you blocked it" or "low vibration" talk. End with one question I should ask myself before next month.

**9 · Mine or the feed's?** (belief 7: your dream life should be yours)
> Here's my vision board list: [paste]. Go through it one item at a time and ask me: would I still want this if nobody ever saw it? Then sort the list into "mine" and "picked up from the feed", and tell me which "mine" item has the smallest first step.

**10 · My Sunday proof list** (belief 6: you're manifesting before you possess it)
> Every Sunday I'll paste last week's list and tell you 3 things that changed this week (a conversation, a skill, an application, a "no" I said). Add them, keep the list in date order, and tell me in one sentence what is more possible for me now than a month ago. Here's last week's list: [paste, or "first week"].

**Prompt hygiene note for the pins/captions (trust):** "Don't paste passwords, bank details or anything you'd hate to see leaked." One line, once per carousel.

**Trust note (applies to every format):** if an image was AI-made, don't present it as a real person's results. No "I manifested this", no before/after, no "this is my life now" under an AI photo. Megan's rule (Nov 16, "Don't label them"): no AI flag in Metricool. Format B shows the prompt next to its image, so it already says plainly the image was made with AI. The prompts are free to use and promise nothing beyond what they do (write a scene, a letter, a list).

**Public-copy rule (lessons, Oct 31):** never name our own research tools in captions or pins. Naming ChatGPT is fine: it's the reader's tool and the search term.
