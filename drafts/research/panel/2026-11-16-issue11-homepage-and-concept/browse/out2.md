# PART 2: THE CONCEPT

Okay, hearing it from a friend, it clicks more than the site did.

**What clicks:**
- "Process, not outcome." Yeah. That's honestly how I think about inspired action. The site made it sound like it was against manifesting. Explained this way, it's not.
- No streaks, no guilt. That's huge, since streaks are the reason I quit other apps.
- Scribe being private, with me picking which lines go into the magazine. That respects my writing. The Editor never rewriting my words matters a lot to me.
- Meditations that rehearse the morning, the hard email. That's basically living in the end, just made practical. I'd try those.
- An Issue that fills up over about 3 months from what I actually do. That's like physical proof, a journal of evidence. That's genuinely a nice idea.
- The printed magazine. This is the piece that makes the app feel like not just another screen.

**What doesn't click or I still don't believe:**
- Typing into Scribe on a phone. It won't feel like my notebook. I'd probably keep paper and maybe copy a few lines in, which sort of kills the "journal entries land on a page as you type" thing.
- "Behavior-change psychology". I'd want to know what that actually means. Is it real or marketing?
- AI. Even if optional, I'm not sure how I feel about an AI "Editor" touching my vision of my future life.
- The weekly nudge could just become a to-do list with nicer fonts.
- I don't know if this is for me or for people who like glow-up Instagram content. The target sounds like women who save aesthetic stuff, and I do too, but I don't want it to be a fashion brand.
- Price. I pay for two apps and I'm picky. $39.99 the first year is fine. $69.99 after that is a lot for something I might quietly stop opening.

**What I'd use:** the meditations, the saves-to-magazine thing, the printed issue, the weekly move. **What I wouldn't:** Scribe as my main journal, and probably the Editor chat features beyond layout.
