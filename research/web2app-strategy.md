## The strategy in 12 rules (Igor, Web2Wave)
1. **First screen = the outcome + a question.** "Become the most interesting person in the room" + "3-minute quiz" + an easy first question. Never "Welcome to…".
2. **Easy questions first** (age, yes/no), harder ones later.
3. **Trust early:** social proof on screen 2 so she finishes the quiz.
4. **Reward screens:** a fun "congrats" screen after early answers = a small dose of dopamine, so it doesn't feel like a survey.
5. **Broad options** so everyone finds herself ("what do you want help with").
6. **Sell the value, never the features.** The product is only explained at the paywall.
7. **Implied authority:** "Whose life inspires you?" with famous names, without claiming they're involved.
8. **Labeling:** "Big picture or detail-oriented?" She starts acting like her answer. The next screen replies to *her* answer.
9. **Pain she says yes to, then support** ("I doubt myself after a mistake" → "If you don't make mistakes, you don't make anything"). Many screens are the same pain, rephrased. No judging, like a good psychologist. "It should be a conversation": every answer gets feedback.
10. **Curiosity gap:** show what could be inside (book titles) without listing features. Then a "why does this matter to you?" anchor.
11. **Loader is never empty:** reviews, a sneak peek, plus mini-questions that hint at features ("Do you like to learn by listening?"). Bare progress bars had the highest drop-off.
12. **Before the paywall:** a "your future" screen (a metric / before-after), real-time social proof, then the paywall: big discount (≥50%) + buy button first, then what you get, FAQ, reviews, price again. Close = downsell.
Also: long is fine (Headway ≈ 50 screens) when the purchase is emotional · don't reinvent the UI (Headway's chatbot-style funnel test disappeared after a month) · intro price that renews higher; Igor prefers no free trial (a tiny first price instead) · per-day pricing works best · up to 8 win-back emails, growing discounts, max 2–3 days apart.

## ISSUE11 guardrails (rules win over tactics)
- **True claims only** (Megan, Sep 24): no "40 million users", no static "5 million in your location", no press or university logos without real coverage or a real cited study.
- **No outcome promises** (App Store 1.4.1 / our voice rules). Pain stays warm, never shaming.
- **In-app, not web, for V1.** ISSUE11 V1 is an iOS app, so payments go through Apple (RevenueCat). Igor's "charge the saved card without asking" works only on the web, and we already ruled it out ("each charge needs its own tap"). A web funnel is a separate, later decision (Revenue + Megan + legal).
- **Countdowns only if the offer really expires** for that user.
- **AI disclosure stays** on Meet Lucky Star (needed when her data goes to an AI service).
